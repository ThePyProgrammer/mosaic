\title{Collaborative Gaussian SLAM for Multi-Robot Mapping: A Survey of Online Local-to-Global Systems}

\author[1]{Prannaya Gupta}
\affil[1]{Pragnition Labs}

\date{May 2026}

\newcolumntype{Y}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X}
\newcommand{\targetclass}{multi-robot online Gaussian SLAM}
\newcommand{\systemname}[1]{\textsc{#1}}

\begin{abstract}
Multi-robot Gaussian SLAM has emerged as a narrow but important variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting research: multiple independently moving robots or agents incrementally estimate local motion and local map state, then apply global alignment and global fusion to observations, local maps, Gaussian submaps, keyframes, or Gaussian primitives to form a global Gaussian or Gaussian-like map. This survey examines seven current systems: public or partly public implementation candidates MAGiC-SLAM, MAC-Ego3D, CoKo-SLAM, and CoMA-SLAM; and paper-only or not-yet-auditable systems HAMMER, GRAND-SLAM, and MAGS-SLAM. The main finding is architectural rather than leaderboard-based. Current systems are organized by fusion pattern---central-server global fusion, distributed Gaussian consensus, submap-plus-pose-graph consistency, monocular compact-submap fusion, and heterogeneous semantic global mapping---while benchmark evidence remains uneven. Accuracy and rendering metrics are commonly reported on Replica-derived multi-agent benchmarks, but communication, memory, merge latency, and scaling evidence are still sparse and not directly comparable across papers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has quickly become a practical scene representation for differentiable rendering and dense visual mapping. In robotics, the hard question is not only whether a single camera can maintain a local Gaussian map, but whether multiple moving robots can contribute bounded online updates to a global map without collapsing under frame-alignment errors, duplicate primitives, network load, or implementation-specific runtime constraints.

This survey focuses on systems that satisfy a functional target: multiple independently moving agents, online or incremental operation, Gaussian or Gaussian-like local map state, a global fusion mechanism, and explicit cross-agent alignment. That target is narrower than ordinary 3DGS reconstruction and broader than papers whose title contains the word ``SLAM.'' It includes RGB-D multi-agent Gaussian SLAM systems such as MAGiC-SLAM and GRAND-SLAM \citep{yugay2025magic_slam,thomas2025grand_slam}, consensus-oriented systems such as MAC-Ego3D \citep{xu2024mac_ego3d}, communication-efficient compact-map systems such as CoKo-SLAM and CoMA-SLAM \citep{li2026coko_slam,chen2026coma_slam}, heterogeneous semantic mapping systems such as HAMMER \citep{yu2025hammer}, and provisional monocular multi-agent systems such as MAGS-SLAM \citep{cao2026mags_slam}.

\section{Setting the Stage}

Multi-robot SLAM is usually formulated as the joint estimation of robot trajectories and an environment representation from sensor measurements. Classical visual and visual-inertial systems express this through poses, landmarks, keyframes, and loop-closure factors; ORB-SLAM3 and Kimera-Multi are representative references for visual multi-map and distributed multi-robot evaluation practice \citep{campos2021orb_slam3,tian2023kimera_multi}. Gaussian SLAM changes the map variable. The global state is not only a sparse feature graph or occupancy map, but a differentiable set of Gaussian primitives whose geometry, appearance, visibility, uncertainty, and provenance may all change during online collaboration.

Let the set of agents be
\[
\mathcal{A}=\{1,\ldots,N\}, \qquad N\geq2,
\]
and let $t\in\mathbb{N}$ index local update time. The paper uses $\mathcal{F}_0$ for a global or server frame and $\mathcal{F}_{i,t}$ for the current local camera, body, or active Gaussian-submap frame of agent $i$. A rigid transform
\[
\mathbf{T}_{b\leftarrow a}\in SE(3)
\]
maps a point expressed in frame $\mathcal{F}_a$ into frame $\mathcal{F}_b$. Thus $\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}$ maps agent $i$'s local coordinates at time $t$ into the global frame. Local tracking estimates either this transform directly or a local-frame trajectory
\[
\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}
=\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,1},\ldots,
\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}\},
\]
where $\mathcal{F}_{\ell_i}$ is a local reference frame that must later be aligned to $\mathcal{F}_0$ by global tracking or global alignment.

The observation history for agent $i$ is
\[
\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}=\{z_{i,1},\ldots,z_{i,t}\}.
\]
A single observation is method-dependent. For RGB-D systems one may write
\[
z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}}=(I_{i,t},D_{i,t},\mathbf{K}_i),
\]
where $I_{i,t}:\Omega_i\rightarrow[0,1]^3$ is color, $D_{i,t}:\Omega_i\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}$ is depth, and $\mathbf{K}_i$ contains intrinsics. Monocular systems omit $D_{i,t}$ and introduce an unknown or estimated scale $\lambda_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}$. Heterogeneous systems may add local SLAM poses, LiDAR-derived depth, semantic embeddings, or device metadata,
\[
z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{het}}=(I_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},d_{i,t},e_{i,t},\xi_i),
\]
where $d_{i,t}$ denotes optional geometric support, $e_{i,t}$ an optional semantic descriptor, and $\xi_i$ a device or embodiment descriptor.

A Gaussian primitive is written as
\[
g_k=(\boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\mathbf{R}_k,\mathbf{s}_k,o_k,\mathbf{c}_k,\mathbf{h}_k,\pi_k),
\]
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\in\mathbb{R}^3$ is the mean, $\mathbf{R}_k\in SO(3)$ and $\mathbf{s}_k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}$ parameterize anisotropic shape, $o_k\in[0,1]$ is opacity, $\mathbf{c}_k$ denotes color or spherical-harmonic appearance parameters, $\mathbf{h}_k$ is an optional semantic or learned descriptor, and $\pi_k$ records provenance such as source agent, keyframe, or submap identity. A local map is a finite Gaussian or Gaussian-like set
\[
\mathcal{M}_{i,t}=\{g_{i,t}^{1},\ldots,g_{i,t}^{m_{i,t}}\},
\]
possibly partitioned into Gaussian submaps $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$, keyframe supports $\mathcal{K}_{i,t}$, or Gaussian-surfel submaps $\mathcal{S}_{i,t}$. A local mapping function is therefore
\[
\phi_i:\bigl(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t}\bigr)
\mapsto \mathcal{M}_{i,t},
\]
where $\mathcal{P}_{i,t}$ denotes method parameters such as insertion thresholds, photometric losses, depth weights, pruning rules, and keyframe policies.

The terminology in the remainder of the paper follows a local/global split. A \emph{local map} is the full Gaussian state $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ maintained by agent $i$ before cross-agent alignment. A \emph{Gaussian submap} is a bounded partition $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}$ of a local map, while a \emph{Gaussian-surfel submap} is denoted $\mathcal{S}_{i,t}$ when a method uses surfel primitives. The \emph{global map} is always $\mathcal{G}_t$, whether it is held by a central server or distributed across agents. Local tracking and local fusion update $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}$ and $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$; global tracking, global alignment, and global fusion estimate cross-agent transforms and update $\mathcal{G}_t$.

Online collaboration requires bounded communication. Let
\[
p_{i,t}=\mu_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)
\]
be a packet selected under budget $b_i$. Depending on the method, $p_{i,t}$ may contain keyframes, descriptors, compacted Gaussians, Gaussian-submap summaries, loop-closure candidates, semantic embeddings, or local pose fragments. The active communication graph is $\mathcal{H}_t=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{R}_t)$; $\mathcal{N}_t(i)=\{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_t\}$ is the neighbor set for distributed methods.

Cross-agent alignment is represented by a constraint set
\[
\mathcal{C}_t=\{c_{ab}\},\qquad
c_{ab}=(a,b,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b},\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{ab},\tau_{ab}),
\]
where $\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b}\in SE(3)$ is a relative transform, $\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{ab}$ is an information matrix or confidence surrogate, and $\tau_{ab}$ records the time, keyframe, or submap support. Constraints may arise from image retrieval, rendered-depth registration, camera-depth registration, geometric verification, semantic correspondence, consensus factors, or inter-robot loop closure. A generic alignment operator is
\[
\alpha_t:\{p_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}
\mapsto \mathcal{C}_t.
\]

The global map is the output of a global fusion operator
\[
\mathcal{G}_t
=\Psi_t\!\left(\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t_i}\mathcal{M}_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}},
\mathcal{C}_t,
\mathcal{P}_t\right),
\]
where $\mathcal{P}_t$ contains global policies such as merge thresholds, update rates, consensus weights, redundancy tests, semantic-code retention, and communication schedules. Pose-graph methods instantiate $\Psi_t$ through an optimization over submap poses,
\[
\{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\}
=\arg\min_{\{\mathbf{P}_v\}}
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t}
\rho\!\left(\|r_e(\{\mathbf{P}_v\})\|_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}_e}^{2}\right),
\]
whereas consensus methods instantiate it through repeated local and inter-agent updates,
\[
\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}
=C_{\mathrm{inter}}\!\left(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)},
\{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\};\lambda_{\mathrm{inter}}\right).
\]

Finally, reported performance should be read as a vector rather than a single score:
\[
\mathbf{q}=\bigl(\mathrm{ATE},\mathrm{PSNR},\mathrm{SSIM},\mathrm{LPIPS},L_D,
\ell_{\mathrm{track}},\ell_{\mathrm{map}},\ell_{\mathrm{merge}},m_{\mathrm{GPU}},s_{\mathrm{pkt}},r_{\mathrm{net}},N\bigr).
\]
Here $L_D$ is a depth error, $\ell_{\mathrm{track}}$, $\ell_{\mathrm{map}}$, and $\ell_{\mathrm{merge}}$ are tracking, mapping, and merge latencies, $m_{\mathrm{GPU}}$ is peak memory, $s_{\mathrm{pkt}}$ is serialized update size, $r_{\mathrm{net}}$ is communication rate, and $N$ is agent count. Current papers report different projections of $\mathbf{q}$, which is why this survey emphasizes architecture rather than a universal ranking.

A system belongs to \targetclass{} when it satisfies five conditions: $N\geq2$ independently moving agents; online or incremental operation; Gaussian or Gaussian-like local map state; global-map semantics; and a cross-agent alignment mechanism. This excludes static multi-view 3DGS training, ordinary stereo, single-robot multi-camera rigs, and independent per-agent mapping without global Gaussian-map semantics.

\section{Existing Works}

The following systems are summarized as survey-level procedures over the notation above. The algorithm boxes are not executable source code; they identify the main mathematical objects each method estimates, communicates, aligns, and fuses.

\subsection{MAGiC-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:magic-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} MAGiC-SLAM is a server-mediated RGB-D pipeline: agents perform local tracking, update local Gaussian maps, partition them into Gaussian submaps, send features or Gaussian submaps to a server, and the server performs global alignment plus global fusion before merging those submaps \citep{yugay2025magic_slam}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/VladimirYugay/MAGiC-SLAM}} The central mechanism is not raw image fusion; it is \emph{Gaussian-submap plus loop-constraint} global fusion.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAGiC-SLAM}
\label{alg:magic-slam}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} RGB-D streams $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}$, local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1}\}$, Gaussian submaps $\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}$, keyframe sets $\{\mathcal{K}_{i,t}\}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$ and optimized Gaussian-submap poses $\{\mathbf{P}_v\}$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Estimate the local tracking transform by photometric--depth registration against the previous local Gaussian state,
  \[
  \hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}
  =\arg\min_{\mathbf{T}\in SE(3)}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rgbd}}\!\left(z_{i,t},\mathrm{render}(\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1},\mathbf{T})\right).
  \]
  \item Update the local map with the survey-level mapping operator,
  $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}=\phi_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$,
  and refresh the active Gaussian submap $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ under the method's submap policy.
  \item Select and send $p_{i,t}=\mu_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$ to the server.
  \item Form loop constraints $\mathcal{C}_t\leftarrow\mathcal{C}_{t-1}\cup
  \alpha_t(\{p_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}})$ from verified intra- and inter-agent matches.
  \item Optimize Gaussian-submap poses,
  \[
  \{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\}=\arg\min_{\{\mathbf{P}_v\}}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t}\rho\!\left(\|r_e(\{\mathbf{P}_v\})\|^2\right).
  \]
  \item Merge optimized Gaussian submaps into $\mathcal{G}_t=\Psi_t(\{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\mathcal{B}_v\},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} Reported ReplicaMultiagent results include about $0.27\,\mathrm{cm}$ ATE RMSE, $34.26\,\mathrm{dB}$ PSNR, $1.12\,\mathrm{GiB}$ peak GPU memory, $54\,\mathrm{MB}$ submaps, and $167\,\mathrm{s}$ global merge time. These figures make MAGiC-SLAM a mature public research baseline, but they also show that global merging can dominate runtime.

\subsection{MAC-Ego3D}
\label{sec:systems:mac-ego3d}

\textbf{Mechanics.} MAC-Ego3D replaces a single authoritative merge step with Gaussian consensus. Each RGB-D agent maintains a local Gaussian map; nearby Gaussians are regularized within an agent, and asynchronous inter-agent consensus aligns and refines the global structure \citep{xu2024mac_ego3d}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Xiaohao-Xu/MAC-Ego3D}} The key object is the repeated consensus update, not a one-shot server merge.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAC-Ego3D}
\label{alg:mac-ego3d}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} RGB-D streams $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}$, local Gaussian maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1}\}$, communication graph $\mathcal{H}_t$, consensus round $h$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Consensus-refined local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}\}$ and an eventually consistent global map $\mathcal{G}_t$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Estimate local motion $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}$ and extend $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}$ from RGB-D tracking.
  \item Form the provisional local map
  $\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t}=\phi_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$.
  \item Regularize nearby local Gaussians by intra-agent consensus,
  $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}}=C_{\mathrm{intra}}(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t};\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}})$.
  \item Select $p_{i,t}^{(h)}=\mu_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},h;b_i)$ for neighbor exchange.
  \item Receive $\{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\}$ over $\mathcal{H}_t$.
  \item Apply inter-agent Gaussian consensus,
  \[
  \mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}=C_{\mathrm{inter}}\!\left(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}},\{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\};\lambda_{\mathrm{inter}}\right).
  \]
  \item Define the distributed global state as $\mathcal{G}_t=\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}:i\in\mathcal{A}\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} Extracted results report about $0.14\,\mathrm{cm}$ ATE RMSE on Multi-Agent Replica, $40.04\,\mathrm{dB}$ PSNR, $0.02\,\mathrm{s}$ tracking, and $0.05\,\mathrm{s}$ mapping. Communication is reported as intervals rather than bytes.

\subsection{CoKo-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:coko-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} CoKo-SLAM keeps the server pattern but changes what is transmitted: compact keyframe-optimized Gaussian/keyframe state instead of dense raw streams \citep{li2026coko_slam}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/lemonci/coko-slam}} The server uses compact map information, loop closure without an initial relative-pose guess, redundant-Gaussian pruning, and global optimization.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{CoKo-SLAM}
\label{alg:coko-slam}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} RGB-D local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\}$, keyframes $\{\mathcal{K}_{i,t}\}$, budgets $\{b_i\}$, previous global map $\mathcal{G}_{t-1}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Compact globally optimized Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Estimate the local pose $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}$, extend $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}$, and update keyframes $\mathcal{K}_{i,t}$.
  \item Update the local map
  $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}=\phi_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$.
  \item Select a compact packet
  \[
  p_{i,t}=\arg\max_{p\subseteq(\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t})}\operatorname{info}(p)
  \quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad |p|\leq b_i.
  \]
  \item Score packet pairs and retain candidate associations with descriptor, rendered-depth, or camera-depth support above threshold $\tau_a$.
  \item Estimate relative transforms
  $\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{T}\in SE(3)}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t},\mathbf{T})$ for accepted packet pairs.
  \item Mark redundant primitives $\mathcal{R}_t=\{g\in\cup_i\mathcal{M}_{i,t}:\operatorname{overlap}(g,\mathcal{G}_{t-1})>\tau_o\}$.
  \item Define surviving primitive sets $\mathcal{U}_{i,t}=\{g\in\mathcal{M}_{i,t}:g\notin\mathcal{R}_t\}$.
  \item Fuse compact aligned evidence as
  $\mathcal{G}_t=\Psi_t(\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\mathcal{U}_{i,t}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} The paper reports $85$--$95\%$ lower transmitted data, but the extracted evidence did not include absolute bandwidth. The public repository supports architectural inspection, but the available evidence is not sufficient for an independent reproducibility claim.

\subsection{CoMA-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:coma-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} CoMA-SLAM is a distributed multi-agent Gaussian-surfel system: agents maintain Gaussian-surfel submaps, close intra/inter-agent loops, and run distributed keyframe optimization \citep{chen2026coma_slam}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/npu-chenlin/CoMA-SLAM}} Its representation is 2D Gaussian surfels rather than conventional 3D radiance splats, so it belongs in a broad Gaussian-map survey with that caveat.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{CoMA-SLAM}
\label{alg:coma-slam}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} Agent observations $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}$, Gaussian-surfel submaps $\{\mathcal{S}_{i,t-1}\}$, neighbor sets $\{\mathcal{N}_t(i)\}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Distributed globally consistent Gaussian-surfel map $\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{surfel}}_t$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Update the Gaussian-surfel submap
  $\mathcal{S}_{i,t}\subseteq\phi_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$.
  \item Generate intra-agent and inter-agent loop candidates between keyframes or Gaussian-surfel submaps.
  \item Verify candidates $\mathcal{V}_t$ and update
  $\mathcal{C}_t\leftarrow\mathcal{C}_{t-1}\cup
  \{(k,\ell,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{k\leftarrow\ell},\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{k\ell},\tau_{k\ell}):(k,\ell)\in\mathcal{V}_t\}$.
  \item Exchange $p_{i,t}=\mu_i(\mathcal{S}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{C}_t;b_i)$ with neighbors $\mathcal{N}_t(i)$.
  \item Solve the distributed keyframe/submap optimization
  \[
  \mathcal{Q}_t^\star=\arg\min_{\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{(k,\ell)\in\mathcal{C}_t}\rho\!\left(\|r_{k\ell}(\mathcal{Q})\|^2\right).
  \]
  \item Place aligned Gaussian-surfel submaps in the global state,
  $\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{surfel}}_t=\bigcup_i\mathcal{Q}_t^\star\mathcal{S}_{i,t}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} The AAAI evidence reports improved pose/rendering/geometric consistency and a $99.8\%$ communication-bandwidth reduction claim, but exact numeric tables were not extracted. The repository was public, but no top-level license was found.

\subsection{HAMMER}
\label{sec:systems:hammer}

\textbf{Mechanics.} HAMMER is a server-side heterogeneous semantic mapper. Robots/devices stream local SLAM poses and visual/perception outputs; the server estimates global frame alignment without prior relative poses and trains or updates a metric-semantic global Gaussian map \citep{yu2025hammer,yu2025hammer_project}. The distinctive output is not just geometry, but a semantic Gaussian map usable by downstream agents.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{HAMMER}
\label{alg:hammer}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} Heterogeneous packets $u_{i,t}=(z_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},e_{i,t},\xi_i)$ and previous semantic map $\mathcal{G}_{t-1}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Metric-semantic global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$ and bounded query views $v_t(q)$.\\
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Normalize each packet as $u_{i,t}=(z_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},e_{i,t},\xi_i)$.
  \item Estimate device-to-global alignment
  $\mathbf{A}_{0\leftarrow\ell_i}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{A}\in SE(3)}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}}(\{u_{i,\tau}\}_{\tau\leq t},\mathcal{G}_{t-1},\mathbf{A})$.
  \item Transform observations into the global frame,
  $\tilde{z}_{i,t}=\mathbf{A}_{0\leftarrow\ell_i}\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}z_{i,t}$.
  \item Associate $\tilde{z}_{i,t}$ with supported primitives $g_k$ or initialize new metric-semantic Gaussians.
  \item Update $(g_k,\mathbf{h}_k,\pi_k)$ and compute
  $\mathcal{G}_t=\Psi_t(\mathcal{G}_{t-1},\{\tilde{z}_{i,t}\},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$.
  \item Answer a bounded query view $v_t(q)=Q(\mathcal{G}_t,q)$ over geometry, semantics, or provenance.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} Reported evidence includes Replica PSNR around $37.28\,\mathrm{dB}$, online updates around $15$--$38\,\mathrm{ms/frame}$, alignment around $36\,\mathrm{s}$ per new robot, and $30$--$35\,\mathrm{Mbps}$ per device in hardware tests. No public implementation was found.

\subsection{GRAND-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:grand-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} GRAND-SLAM is best treated as Gaussian submaps plus a global consistency layer. Agents optimize RGB-D Gaussian submaps derived from their local maps; inter- and intra-robot loop closures create constraints; pose-graph optimization updates global-frame consistency \citep{thomas2025grand_slam,thomas2025grand_slam_arxiv}. Do not describe it as CoKo-style centralized rendered-depth fusion unless a source explicitly supports that.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{GRAND-SLAM}
\label{alg:grand-slam}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} RGB-D streams $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}$, active Gaussian submaps $\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}$, loop-candidate database.\\
\textbf{Output:} Globally consistent Gaussian reconstruction $\mathcal{G}_t$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Estimate frame-to-submap pose
  $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathcal{B}_i^m\leftarrow i,t}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{T}\in SE(3)}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rgbd}}(z_{i,t},\mathrm{render}(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m},\mathbf{T}))$.
  \item Spawn $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m+1}$ when drift, spatial extent, or support-frame count exceeds the submap threshold.
  \item Optimize the active Gaussian submap
  $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{i,t}^{m}}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rgbd}}(z,\mathrm{render}(\mathcal{B}))$.
  \item Detect and verify intra-robot and inter-robot loop closures between Gaussian submaps or keyframes.
  \item Solve the submap pose graph with $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t$ to obtain $\{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\}$.
  \item Compose the globally consistent reconstruction
  $\mathcal{G}_t=\bigcup_v\mathbf{P}_v^\star\mathcal{B}_v$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} Extracted results include Multiagent Replica ATE around $0.25\,\mathrm{cm}$ and Kimera-Multi Outdoor six-agent evaluation over about $1.85\,\mathrm{km}$ with average ATE around $4.99\,\mathrm{m}$ \citep{tian2023kimera_multi}. No public code was found in the research pass.

\subsection{MAGS-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:mags-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} MAGS-SLAM is the RGB-only outlier: each monocular agent builds Gaussian submaps from its local map, transmits compact summaries, verifies cross-agent associations with geometry and appearance, and fuses via occupancy-aware Gaussian fusion \citep{cao2026mags_slam,cao2026mags_slam_project}. The core problem is scale-aware collaboration without active depth.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAGS-SLAM}
\label{alg:mags-slam}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} Monocular RGB streams $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}$, local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1}\}$, Gaussian submaps $\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}$, compact-summary budget $b_i$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Scale-aligned collaborative global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Estimate monocular pose $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}$ and scale $\lambda_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}$, then extend $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}$.
  \item Update the local map
  $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}=\phi_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$,
  and refresh the active Gaussian submap $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$.
  \item Select a compact summary
  $p_{i,t}=\mu_i(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$.
  \item Retain cross-agent candidates whose geometric, appearance, and occupancy evidence passes verification.
  \item Estimate scale-aware alignment
  $(\lambda_i^\star,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b}^\star)=\arg\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbf{T}\in SE(3)}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mono}}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t},\lambda,\mathbf{T})$.
  \item Fuse aligned Gaussian submaps with occupancy evidence,
  $\mathcal{G}_t=F_{\mathrm{occ}}(\{\lambda_i^\star\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}_i,\mathcal{O}_{t-1})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} MAGS-SLAM is the leading claimed monocular multi-agent 3DGS SLAM direction in this set, but available evidence is project-page/arXiv-stage. Code and data were reported as release-upon-acceptance, so runtime and bandwidth claims remain provisional.

\section{Overall Architecture Taxonomy}

The systems above can be compared by the factorization
\[
\underbrace{\phi_i}_{\text{local tracking and mapping}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\mu_i}_{\text{update selection}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\alpha_t}_{\text{cross-agent alignment}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\Psi_t}_{\text{global Gaussian fusion}}.
\]
The recurring question is not simply whether a method uses Gaussians. It is where the four responsibilities live, which variables cross the communication boundary, and which component absorbs drift, redundancy, and stale information.

\subsection{Central-Server Submap and Gaussian Fusion}

Central-server systems place most of $\alpha_t$ and $\Psi_t$ on a global mapper. MAGiC-SLAM transmits Gaussian submaps and loop-closure evidence to a server that optimizes Gaussian-submap poses before merging Gaussians. CoKo-SLAM keeps the same broad centralization but reduces $p_{i,t}$ through keyframe-optimized compact packets and redundant-Gaussian pruning. HAMMER also uses a server, but its packet stream is heterogeneous and semantic: the server aligns local SLAM outputs into a global frame and updates a metric-semantic global Gaussian map.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Central-server submap and Gaussian fusion pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-central-server}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} Local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\}$, local trajectories $\{\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}\}$, packets $\{p_{i,t}\}$, previous server-side global map $\mathcal{G}_{t-1}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Server-side global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$ and global-frame agent transforms $\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\}$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Compute local updates $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}=\phi_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$.
  \item Transmit bounded packets $p_{i,t}=\mu_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$ to the server.
  \item Estimate cross-agent constraints
  $\mathcal{C}_t=\alpha_t(\{p_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}})$ by loop closure, rendered-depth alignment, camera-depth alignment, or heterogeneous registration.
  \item Optimize global-frame Gaussian-submap or device transforms $\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\}$ from $\mathcal{C}_t$ and odometry factors.
  \item Remove or downweight redundant primitives
  $\mathcal{R}_t=\{g:\operatorname{support}(g)\cap\operatorname{support}(\mathcal{G}_{t-1})>\tau_o\}$.
  \item Define surviving aligned primitive sets $\mathcal{U}_{i,t_i}=\{g\in\mathcal{M}_{i,t_i}:g\notin\mathcal{R}_t\}$.
  \item Fuse aligned nonredundant geometry, appearance, and optional semantics into
  $\mathcal{G}_t=\Psi_t(\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t_i}\mathcal{U}_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

The advantage of this pattern is a coherent global optimization point. Server-side fusion can compare packets across agents, remove duplicate primitives, and maintain a single $\mathcal{G}_t$. The cost is that merge latency, memory, bandwidth, and single-server availability become first-order variables. MAGiC-SLAM's reported global merge time illustrates this cost, while HAMMER's hardware results are unusually explicit about per-device Mbps streams. Available ablation evidence is mostly about loop closure, alignment, compaction, or baseline comparisons rather than a complete systems ablation over bandwidth and agent count.

\subsection{Distributed Gaussian Consensus}

Distributed Gaussian consensus keeps every $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ active and uses peer updates to move those local maps toward consistency. MAC-Ego3D expresses this pattern most directly: intra-agent consensus regularizes each $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$, while inter-agent consensus uses packets from $\mathcal{N}_t(i)$ to refine cross-agent map agreement. CoMA-SLAM follows a related distributed idea with Gaussian-surfel submaps, loop factors, and distributed keyframe optimization.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Distributed Gaussian consensus pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-distributed-consensus}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} Local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)}\}$, communication graph $\mathcal{H}_t$, neighbor packets $\{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Consensus-refined local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}\}$ and distributed global state $\mathcal{G}_t$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Update local Gaussian or Gaussian-surfel state
  $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)}\leftarrow\phi_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$.
  \item Regularize local primitives or submaps by
  $\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t}^{(h)}=C_{\mathrm{intra}}(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)};\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}})$.
  \item Select $p_{i,t}^{(h)}=\mu_i(\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t}^{(h)},\mathcal{C}_t,h;b_i)$.
  \item Exchange packets with neighbors $\mathcal{N}_t(i)$ under the asynchronous schedule on $\mathcal{H}_t$.
  \item Estimate pairwise alignments or consensus factors and update the local constraint view $\mathcal{C}_{i,t}$.
  \item Compute
  $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}=C_{\mathrm{inter}}(\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t}^{(h)},\{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\};\lambda_{\mathrm{inter}})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

This pattern reduces dependence on a single fusion server and can naturally support asynchronous collaboration. Its hard problems are convergence, conflict resolution, duplicate primitive handling, stale packets, and communication scheduling. Reported evidence supports strong tracking/rendering for MAC-Ego3D and large relative communication reductions for CoMA-SLAM, but byte-level communication, stale-update, and failure-mode ablations remain sparse in the available evidence.

\subsection{Submap-Plus-Pose-Graph Consistency}

Submap-plus-pose-graph systems treat dense Gaussian maps as local products whose global consistency is controlled by a graph over Gaussian-submap poses. GRAND-SLAM is the clearest example: RGB-D Gaussian submaps are optimized independently, loop closures add factors to $\mathcal{C}_t$, and pose-graph optimization updates the transforms that place Gaussian submaps into the global frame. MAGiC-SLAM also uses this pattern inside a centralized architecture, but GRAND-SLAM makes the Gaussian-submap consistency layer the main comparison unit.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Submap-plus-pose-graph consistency pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-submap-pose-graph}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} Gaussian submaps $\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}$, odometry factors $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t$, loop candidates, previous Gaussian-submap poses $\{\mathbf{P}_v\}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Globally consistent Gaussian-submap placement and global map $\mathcal{G}_t$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Estimate observation-to-submap poses and create a new Gaussian-submap node when drift or support exceeds threshold.
  \item Optimize each active Gaussian submap
  $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{i,t}^{m}}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rgbd}}(z,\mathrm{render}(\mathcal{B}))$.
  \item Verify intra-agent and inter-agent loop closures and add relative-pose constraints to $\mathcal{C}_t$.
  \item Build the pose graph $(\mathcal{V}_t,\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t)$ over Gaussian-submap poses.
  \item Solve
  \[
  \{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\}=\arg\min_{\{\mathbf{P}_v\}}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t}
  \rho\!\left(\|r_e(\{\mathbf{P}_v\})\|_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}_e}^{2}\right).
  \]
  \item Compose $\mathcal{G}_t=\bigcup_v\mathbf{P}_v^\star\mathcal{B}_v$ after overlap reconciliation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

The strength of this pattern is that it reuses a mature SLAM abstraction: dense maps can remain local while global corrections act through $\mathbf{P}_v^\star$. The main risk is that a corrected pose graph does not by itself remove duplicate or inconsistent Gaussian primitives. A useful ablation surface is loop closure: GRAND-SLAM reports weaker results without loop closure, which supports the claim that cross-agent consistency factors, not only local photometric mapping, drive the large-scale result.

\subsection{Compact Communication and Monocular Submap Fusion}

Compact communication appears in both RGB-D and monocular systems, but it becomes structurally central for monocular collaboration. CoKo-SLAM uses compaction to reduce transmitted Gaussian/keyframe state while preserving enough information for server-side loop closure and pruning. MAGS-SLAM goes further: each agent has only RGB observations, so $\mu_i$ must produce compact Gaussian-submap summaries that support both cross-agent verification and scale-aware alignment.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Compact communication and monocular submap fusion pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-compact-monocular}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} Local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\}$, keyframes $\{\mathcal{K}_{i,t}\}$, optional scale hypotheses $\{\lambda_{i,t}\}$, packet budgets $\{b_i\}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Bandwidth-limited global map $\mathcal{G}_t$ and verified cross-agent alignments.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Estimate local poses $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}$ and monocular scale hypotheses $\lambda_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}$.
  \item Update local maps $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}=\phi_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$.
  \item Score candidate keyframes, Gaussian submaps, descriptors, and primitives by $\operatorname{info}(\cdot)$ under budget $b_i$.
  \item Select $p_{i,t}=\mu_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$ rather than dense observations.
  \item Verify packet pairs with geometry, appearance, rendered depth, camera depth, or occupancy residuals.
  \item Estimate rigid or scale-aware transforms
  $(\lambda^\star,\Delta\mathbf{T}^\star)=\arg\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbf{T}\in SE(3)}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{align}}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t},\lambda,\mathbf{T})$.
  \item Fuse accepted nonredundant evidence into
  $\mathcal{G}_t=\Psi_t(\{\lambda_i^\star\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

The tradeoff is between informativeness and bandwidth. Small packets help online operation, but if $p_{i,t}$ omits the wrong geometry, appearance, or occupancy evidence, $\alpha_t$ cannot reliably recover the transform. Monocular systems add scale ambiguity, so the alignment problem includes both rigid transform and scale hypotheses. CoKo-SLAM reports large relative transmission reductions; MAGS-SLAM is important architecturally but its code/data and quantitative communication evidence remain provisional in the sources used here.

\subsection{Heterogeneous Semantic Global Gaussian Mapping}

HAMMER expands the global map from geometry and radiance to metric-semantic scene memory. In the notation above, the Gaussian descriptor $\mathbf{h}_k$ and provenance $\pi_k$ are not auxiliary bookkeeping; they are part of what makes $\mathcal{G}_t$ useful to heterogeneous robots and downstream queries. The alignment operator $\alpha_t$ must reconcile device-specific local SLAM outputs, sensor modalities, and asynchronous packet timing before semantic Gaussian updates are meaningful.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Heterogeneous semantic global Gaussian mapping pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-heterogeneous-semantic}
\small
\noindent\textbf{Input:} Heterogeneous streams $\{z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{het}}\}$, local SLAM poses $\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}\}$, semantic descriptors $\{e_{i,t}\}$, previous semantic map $\mathcal{G}_{t-1}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} Metric-semantic global map $\mathcal{G}_t$ with provenance-aware query views.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Encode heterogeneous observations as $z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{het}}=(I_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},d_{i,t},e_{i,t},\xi_i)$.
  \item Estimate device-to-global alignments
  $\mathbf{A}_{0\leftarrow\ell_i}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{A}\in SE(3)}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{het}}(\{z_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{het}}\}_{\tau\leq t},\mathcal{G}_{t-1},\mathbf{A})$.
  \item Transform observations into $\mathcal{F}_0$ and associate them with supported primitives or new candidates.
  \item Update $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\mathbf{R}_k,\mathbf{s}_k,o_k,\mathbf{c}_k,\mathbf{h}_k,\pi_k)$ for associated primitives.
  \item Prune, merge, or retain conflicting primitive evidence by uncertainty, source support, and semantic consistency.
  \item Return bounded views $v_t(q)=Q(\mathcal{G}_t,q)$ over $\mathcal{G}_t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

This pattern is attractive because robot teams rarely have identical sensors or roles. A global map that contains semantics, source provenance, and queryable local evidence can support navigation, inspection, and human-robot interaction more directly than a purely photometric reconstruction. The open ablation surface is correspondingly broader: papers need to isolate the value of semantic descriptors, frame-alignment design, device heterogeneity, and communication choices rather than reporting only rendering quality or depth error.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

The main lesson from current multi-robot Gaussian SLAM is that architecture matters more than a single metric row. Local tracking and local mapping $\phi_i$, update selection $\mu_i$, global alignment $\alpha_t$, and global fusion $\Psi_t$ are separable responsibilities with different failure modes. A paper can report strong PSNR or ATE while still leaving open whether it scales in bandwidth, memory, merge latency, stale updates, or heterogeneous deployments.

A useful next step for the field is standardized systems reporting. Alongside ATE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and depth error, papers should report serialized packet size, communication rate, per-update latency, global merge latency, peak memory, map size, agent count, trajectory length, and behavior under delayed or dropped messages. These quantities should be separated by component: tracking should not be conflated with mapping, mapping should not be conflated with global alignment, and global alignment should not be conflated with final rendering quality.

The architecture taxonomy also suggests a modular research path. Central-server methods should be stress-tested for merge bottlenecks and server failure; distributed consensus methods should be evaluated under asynchronous and stale communication; submap-plus-pose-graph methods should report how dense Gaussian primitives are reconciled after graph corrections; compact-submap methods should expose the information lost by compression; semantic global-map methods should report provenance and uncertainty, not only photometric fidelity.

Multi-robot online Gaussian SLAM is therefore no longer speculative, but it remains immature as a systems discipline. MAGiC-SLAM and MAC-Ego3D are strong public baselines for centralized and consensus-oriented designs; CoKo-SLAM and CoMA-SLAM highlight communication-efficient directions with reproducibility caveats; HAMMER, GRAND-SLAM, and MAGS-SLAM expand the design space toward heterogeneous semantics, large-scale consistency, and monocular collaboration. Future progress will likely come from treating these patterns as composable architectural choices and evaluating each choice against the operational constraints of local-to-global robotic mapping.

\begin{abstract}
Multi-robot Gaussian SLAM has emerged as a narrow but strategically important variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting research: multiple independently moving robots or agents incrementally estimate local motion and map state, then fuse observations, submaps, keyframes, or Gaussian primitives into a shared Gaussian or Gaussian-like map. This survey summarizes seven current candidates relevant to Mosaic backend planning: public or partly public implementation candidates MAGiC-SLAM, MAC-Ego3D, CoKo-SLAM, and CoMA-SLAM; and paper-only or not-yet-auditable systems HAMMER, GRAND-SLAM, and MAGS-SLAM. The main finding is architectural rather than leaderboard-based. The field is organized by fusion contracts---central server fusion, distributed Gaussian consensus, submap-plus-pose-graph consistency, monocular compact-submap fusion, and heterogeneous semantic shared mapping---while benchmark evidence remains uneven. Accuracy and rendering metrics are commonly reported on Replica-derived multi-agent benchmarks, but communication, memory, merge latency, and scaling evidence are still sparse and not directly comparable across papers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has quickly become a practical scene representation for differentiable rendering and dense visual mapping. In robotics, the hard question is not only whether a single camera can maintain a Gaussian map, but whether multiple moving robots can contribute bounded online updates to a shared map without collapsing under frame-alignment errors, duplicate primitives, network load, or backend-specific CUDA dependencies. This question matters for Mosaic because the library aims to expose typed, headless contracts for multi-view and multi-robot Gaussian SLAM without embedding one research backend as a hard dependency.

This survey focuses on systems that satisfy a functional target: multiple independently moving agents, online or incremental operation, Gaussian or Gaussian-like map state, a shared-map fusion mechanism, and explicit cross-agent alignment. That target is narrower than ordinary 3DGS reconstruction and broader than papers whose title contains the word ``SLAM.'' It includes RGB-D multi-agent Gaussian SLAM systems such as MAGiC-SLAM and GRAND-SLAM \citep{yugay2025magic_slam,thomas2025grand_slam}, consensus-oriented systems such as MAC-Ego3D \citep{xu2024mac_ego3d}, communication-efficient compact-map systems such as CoKo-SLAM and CoMA-SLAM \citep{li2026coko_slam,chen2026coma_slam}, heterogeneous semantic mapping systems such as HAMMER \citep{yu2025hammer}, and provisional monocular multi-agent systems such as MAGS-SLAM \citep{cao2026mags_slam}.


\section{Problem Statement and Target Class}

We define \targetclass{} as a class of systems in which multiple independently moving agents incrementally estimate local motion and map state while contributing to a shared Gaussian or Gaussian-like representation. Let the robot team be
\[
\mathcal{A}=\{1,\ldots,N\}, \qquad N\geq 2,
\]
where each agent $i\in\mathcal{A}$ has a local body or camera frame $\mathcal{F}_{i,t}$ at time $t$, a local trajectory estimate $\mathbf{T}_{i,t}\in SE(3)$, and a stream of observations
\[
\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}=\{z_{i,1},\ldots,z_{i,t}\}.
\]
Each observation may contain RGB, RGB-D, monocular RGB, stereo, LiDAR-derived depth, inertial cues, local SLAM poses, or semantic features depending on the system. A local online mapping backend produces a time-indexed Gaussian submap
\[
\mathcal{M}_{i,t}=\phi_i\!\left(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\mathbf{T}_{i,1:t}\right),
\]
where $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ may include 3D Gaussian splats, 2D Gaussian surfels, keyframes, descriptors, semantic embeddings, and provenance. The shared map is obtained through a fusion operator
\[
\mathcal{G}_t
=\Psi_t\!\left(\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t_i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t\right),
\]
where $\mathcal{C}_t$ denotes cross-agent constraints such as frame alignments, inter-robot loop closures, consensus factors, or pose-graph edges, and $\mathcal{P}_t$ denotes policy variables such as communication budgets, keyframe selection, compaction, confidence thresholds, and semantic-code retention.

A system is target-class when it satisfies five conditions: $N\geq2$ independently moving agents; online or incremental operation; Gaussian or Gaussian-like map state; shared-map semantics; and a cross-agent alignment mechanism. This definition excludes static multi-view 3DGS training, ordinary stereo, single-robot multi-camera rigs, and independent per-agent mapping without shared Gaussian-map semantics.

\section{Background and Related Work}

Multi-robot SLAM predates Gaussian Splatting by decades and has mature precedents in feature-based, visual-inertial, and distributed mapping systems. ORB-SLAM3, for example, provides a strong open-source visual and visual-inertial multi-map baseline, but it is not a Gaussian-map system \citep{campos2021orb_slam3}. Kimera-Multi provides important multi-robot dataset and distributed-SLAM context for evaluating collaborative mapping systems \citep{tian2023kimera_multi}. Replica-derived datasets provide the indoor synthetic scenes from which several multi-agent Gaussian SLAM benchmarks are built \citep{straub2019replica,replica_multiagent_dataset}.

Gaussian representations change the collaborative SLAM contract. In sparse or mesh-based systems, inter-robot fusion can often be described as keyframe exchange, loop closure, and pose-graph optimization. In Gaussian-map systems, a backend must also decide how to transmit, align, merge, prune, deduplicate, and update dense differentiable primitives. A shared map may be a server-trained semantic Gaussian map, a set of aligned local submaps, a consensus-optimized Gaussian field, or a compact monocular submap product. The same terms---``online'', ``real time'', ``multi-agent'', and ``fusion''---therefore hide different system boundaries across papers.

\section{Survey Method and Evidence Criteria}

The survey synthesizes previously completed research outputs, paper/project pages, public repositories, and extracted benchmark tables. No benchmark numbers in this paper are reproduced measurements. They are reported values from the cited papers, project pages, repositories, or prior survey extraction artifacts. For integration planning, each system is assigned an evidence tier:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Public implementation evidence:} a repository was found and is relevant to the method, but the code may still be research-oriented and dependency-heavy.
  \item \textbf{Public with reuse caveats:} a repository exists, but licensing, dependency hygiene, or provenance makes reuse uncertain.
  \item \textbf{Paper-only / project-page-only evidence:} the system is target-class by paper claims, but no auditable implementation was found.
  \item \textbf{Provisional evidence:} the system is claimed or promised, but code, data, or final archival details remain incomplete.
\end{itemize}

This evidence model is intentionally conservative. It separates architecture evidence from backend adoption evidence, and it treats relative communication reductions as method-specific paper claims unless absolute byte counts or bandwidth tables are available.


\section{Candidate Systems}

Each system below is summarized as a concrete dataflow: what runs on each robot, what crosses the network, what alignment object is estimated, and what becomes the shared Gaussian map. The algorithm boxes are evidence-bounded sequential flows: each numbered stage advances a tracker, states its trigger, applies a named update, and emits the artifact consumed by the next stage.

\subsection{MAGiC-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:magic-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} MAGiC-SLAM is a server-mediated RGB-D pipeline: agents track locally, build local Gaussian submaps, send features/submaps to a server, and the server uses loop closure plus pose-graph optimization before merging submaps \citep{yugay2025magic_slam}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/VladimirYugay/MAGiC-SLAM}.} The important contract is not raw image fusion; it is \emph{submap plus loop constraint} fusion.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAGiC-SLAM sequential server-fusion flow}
\label{alg:magic-slam}
\small
\textbf{State:} agent $x_{i,t}=(\mathbf{T}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t})$; server $X_t=(\mathcal{V}_t,\mathcal{E}_t,\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{G}_t)$; stage tracker $s=0\rightarrow5$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{s=\arabic*},nosep]
  \item \textsc{Track}. \emph{Trigger:} RGB-D frame $z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}}$. \emph{Update:} $\mathbf{T}_{i,t}\leftarrow f_{\theta}^{\mathrm{track}}(z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}},\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{i,t})$. \emph{Emits:} pose $\mathbf{T}_{i,t}$.
  \item \textsc{Local map}. \emph{Trigger:} accepted pose. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\leftarrow u_{\theta}^{\mathrm{gs}}(\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1},z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}},\mathbf{T}_{i,t})$. \emph{Emits:} local submap $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$.
  \item \textsc{Upload}. \emph{Trigger:} selected keyframe/submap. \emph{Update:} $q_{i,t}\leftarrow\mu(\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t})$ and $X_t\leftarrow X_{t-1}\cup q_{i,t}$. \emph{Emits:} server packet $q_{i,t}$.
  \item \textsc{Loop constraints}. \emph{Trigger:} packet-pair score $>\tau_{\mathrm{lc}}$. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{C}_t\leftarrow\{(a,b,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{ab})\}$. \emph{Emits:} inter/intra-agent constraints.
  \item \textsc{Pose graph}. \emph{Trigger:} changed constraint set. \emph{Update:} $\{\mathbf{P}_v\}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathbf{P}}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t}\rho(\|r_e(\mathbf{P})\|^2)$. \emph{Emits:} optimized submap poses.
  \item \textsc{Merge}. \emph{Trigger:} optimized poses. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{G}_t\leftarrow\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{V}_t}\mathbf{P}_v\mathcal{M}_v$. \emph{Emits:} global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} Reported ReplicaMultiagent results include about $0.27\,\mathrm{cm}$ ATE RMSE, $34.26\,\mathrm{dB}$ PSNR, $1.12\,\mathrm{GiB}$ peak GPU memory, $54\,\mathrm{MB}$ submaps, and $167\,\mathrm{s}$ global merge time. Treat it as a mature public research baseline, not a clean Mosaic dependency.

\subsection{MAC-Ego3D}
\label{sec:systems:mac-ego3d}

\textbf{Mechanics.} MAC-Ego3D replaces a single authoritative merge step with Gaussian consensus. Each RGB-D agent maintains a local Gaussian map; nearby Gaussians are regularized within an agent, and asynchronous inter-agent consensus aligns and refines the shared structure \citep{xu2024mac_ego3d}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Xiaohao-Xu/MAC-Ego3D}.} The key object is the repeated consensus update, not a one-shot server merge.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAC-Ego3D sequential consensus flow}
\label{alg:mac-ego3d}
\small
\textbf{State:} agent $x_{i,t}=(\mathbf{T}_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t},h_{i,t})$; communication gate $c_t=\mathbb{1}[t\bmod T_{\mathrm{comm}}=0]$; stage tracker $s=0\rightarrow4$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{s=\arabic*},nosep]
  \item \textsc{Track}. \emph{Trigger:} RGB-D frame $z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}}$. \emph{Update:} $(\mathbf{T}_{i,t},\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t})\leftarrow f_\theta(z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}},\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1})$. \emph{Emits:} pose and provisional Gaussians.
  \item \textsc{Intra-agent consensus}. \emph{Trigger:} local primitive update. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}}\leftarrow C_{\mathrm{intra}}(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t};\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}})$. \emph{Emits:} locally coherent map.
  \item \textsc{Exchange gate}. \emph{Trigger:} $c_t=1$; otherwise skip to publish. \emph{Update:} $B_{i,t}\leftarrow\mu(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}},h_{i,t})$. \emph{Emits:} consensus packet $B_{i,t}$.
  \item \textsc{Inter-agent consensus}. \emph{Trigger:} neighbor packets from $\mathcal{N}_t(i)$. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\leftarrow C_{\mathrm{inter}}(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}},\{B_{j,t}\};\lambda_{\mathrm{inter}})$ and $h_{i,t}\leftarrow h_{i,t-1}+1$. \emph{Emits:} cross-agent refined map.
  \item \textsc{Publish view}. \emph{Trigger:} local or inter-agent consensus done. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{G}_t\leftarrow\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)}\}_{i,h}$. \emph{Emits:} eventually consistent shared model.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} Extracted results report about $0.14\,\mathrm{cm}$ ATE RMSE on Multi-Agent Replica, $40.04\,\mathrm{dB}$ PSNR, $0.02\,\mathrm{s}$ tracking, and $0.05\,\mathrm{s}$ mapping. Communication is reported as intervals rather than bytes.

\subsection{CoKo-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:coko-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} CoKo-SLAM keeps the server pattern but changes what is transmitted: compact keyframe-optimized Gaussian/keyframe state instead of dense raw streams \citep{li2026coko_slam}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/lemonci/coko-slam}.} The server uses compact map information, loop closure without an initial relative-pose guess, redundant-Gaussian pruning, and global optimization.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{CoKo-SLAM sequential compact-server flow}
\label{alg:coko-slam}
\small
\textbf{State:} local $x_{i,t}=(\mathbf{T}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t})$; packet $p_{i,t}=(\bar{\mathcal{K}}_{i,t},\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t},d_{i,t})$; server $Y_t=(\mathcal{A}_t,\mathcal{R}_t,\mathcal{G}_t)$; stage tracker $s=0\rightarrow5$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{s=\arabic*},nosep]
  \item \textsc{Local map}. \emph{Trigger:} RGB-D segment. \emph{Update:} refresh $\mathbf{T}_{i,t}$, $\mathcal{K}_{i,t}$, and $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$. \emph{Emits:} robot-side map/keyframe state.
  \item \textsc{Compact}. \emph{Trigger:} upload window or keyframe event. \emph{Update:} $p_{i,t}\leftarrow\arg\max_{p\subseteq(\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t})}\operatorname{info}(p)$ subject to $|p|\le b_i$. \emph{Emits:} compact packet.
  \item \textsc{Associate}. \emph{Trigger:} server has at least two packets. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{A}_t\leftarrow\{(p_a,p_b):\operatorname{desc}(p_a,p_b)>\tau_d\}$. \emph{Emits:} candidate cross-agent pairs.
  \item \textsc{Verify and align}. \emph{Trigger:} candidate pair. \emph{Update:} $\Delta\mathbf{T}_{ab}\leftarrow\operatorname{align}_m(p_a,p_b)$ for $m\in\{\textsc{RenderedDepth},\textsc{CameraDepth}\}$. \emph{Emits:} verified transform.
  \item \textsc{Prune}. \emph{Trigger:} aligned packet overlaps map. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{R}_t\leftarrow\{g:\operatorname{overlap}(g)>\tau_o\}$. \emph{Emits:} redundant Gaussian set.
  \item \textsc{Optimize and fuse}. \emph{Trigger:} constraints and prune set fixed. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{G}_t\leftarrow\operatorname{opt}((\bigcup_i\Delta\mathbf{T}_i\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t})\setminus\mathcal{R}_t)$. \emph{Emits:} compact aligned map.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} The paper reports $85$--$95\%$ lower transmitted data, but the extracted evidence did not include absolute bandwidth. The public code is useful evidence for architecture, but dependency hygiene is rough.

\subsection{CoMA-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:coma-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} CoMA-SLAM is a distributed multi-agent Gaussian-surfel system: agents maintain submap-centric updates, close intra/inter-agent loops, and run distributed keyframe optimization \citep{chen2026coma_slam}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/npu-chenlin/CoMA-SLAM}.} Its representation is 2D Gaussian surfels rather than conventional 3D radiance splats, so it belongs in a broad Gaussian-map survey with that caveat.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{CoMA-SLAM sequential distributed-surfel flow}
\label{alg:coma-slam}
\small
\textbf{State:} peer $x_{i,t}=(\mathbf{T}_{i,t},\mathcal{S}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{L}_{i,t})$; peer graph $\mathcal{N}_t$; stage tracker $s=0\rightarrow4$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{s=\arabic*},nosep]
  \item \textsc{Surfel submap}. \emph{Trigger:} observation $z_{i,t}$. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{S}_{i,t}\leftarrow U_{\mathrm{surfel}}(\mathcal{S}_{i,t-1},z_{i,t},\mathbf{T}_{i,t})$. \emph{Emits:} local Gaussian-surfel submap.
  \item \textsc{Loop detect}. \emph{Trigger:} new keyframe/submap. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{L}_{i,t}\leftarrow\mathcal{L}_{i,t-1}\cup\{(k,\ell,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{k\ell}):\operatorname{loop}(k,\ell)>\tau_\ell\}$. \emph{Emits:} loop-factor set.
  \item \textsc{Share}. \emph{Trigger:} peer $j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)$ and budget $b_i$. \emph{Update:} $m_{i\rightarrow j,t}\leftarrow\mu(\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{L}_{i,t},\mathcal{S}_{i,t};b_i)$. \emph{Emits:} compact distributed update.
  \item \textsc{Distributed optimize}. \emph{Trigger:} peer loop factors received. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{Q}_t\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{(k,\ell)\in\cup_i\mathcal{L}_{i,t}}\rho(\|r_{k\ell}(\mathcal{Q})\|^2)$. \emph{Emits:} optimized keyframe/submap poses.
  \item \textsc{Commit map}. \emph{Trigger:} distributed optimization converged. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{G}_t^{\mathrm{surfel}}\leftarrow\bigcup_i\mathcal{Q}_t\mathcal{S}_{i,t}$. \emph{Emits:} shared Gaussian-surfel map.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} The AAAI evidence reports improved pose/rendering/geometric consistency and a $99.8\%$ communication-bandwidth reduction claim, but exact numeric tables were not extracted. The repository was public, but no top-level license was found.

\subsection{HAMMER}
\label{sec:systems:hammer}

\textbf{Mechanics.} HAMMER is a server-side heterogeneous semantic mapper. Robots/devices stream local SLAM poses and visual/perception outputs; the server estimates global frame alignment without prior relative poses and trains or updates a shared metric-semantic 3D Gaussian map \citep{yu2025hammer,yu2025hammer_project}. The distinctive output is not just geometry, but a semantic Gaussian map usable by downstream agents.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{HAMMER sequential heterogeneous-semantic flow}
\label{alg:hammer}
\small
\textbf{State:} device packet $u_{i,t}=(z_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{local}},e_{i,t})$; transform $\mathbf{A}_{i,t}:\mathcal{F}_i\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_G$; semantic Gaussians $(g_k,c_k,\pi_k)$; stage tracker $s=0\rightarrow4$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{s=\arabic*},nosep]
  \item \textsc{Stream packet}. \emph{Trigger:} robot/device sends $u_{i,t}$. \emph{Update:} buffer observation, local pose, and embedding under device id $i$. \emph{Emits:} time-stamped device packet.
  \item \textsc{Register}. \emph{Trigger:} new device or alignment check. \emph{Update:} $\mathbf{A}_{i,t}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathbf{A}\in SE(3)}D(\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{i,1:t}^{\mathrm{local}},\mathcal{G}_{t-1})$. \emph{Emits:} global-frame transform.
  \item \textsc{Assimilate}. \emph{Trigger:} transform available. \emph{Update:} $\tilde{z}_{i,t}\leftarrow\mathbf{A}_{i,t}\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{local}}z_{i,t}$. \emph{Emits:} global-frame observation.
  \item \textsc{Semantic update}. \emph{Trigger:} observation projects into map support. \emph{Update:} $(g_k,c_k,\pi_k)\leftarrow U_{\mathrm{sem}}((g_k,c_k,\pi_k),\tilde{z}_{i,t},e_{i,t},i,t)$. \emph{Emits:} updated metric-semantic Gaussians.
  \item \textsc{Serve query}. \emph{Trigger:} downstream query $q$. \emph{Update:} $v_t(q)\leftarrow Q(\mathcal{G}_t,q)$. \emph{Emits:} geometry, semantic, or provenance view.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} Reported evidence includes Replica PSNR around $37.28\,\mathrm{dB}$, online updates around $15$--$38\,\mathrm{ms/frame}$, alignment around $36\,\mathrm{s}$ per new robot, and $30$--$35\,\mathrm{Mbps}$ per device in hardware tests. No public implementation was found.

\subsection{GRAND-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:grand-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} GRAND-SLAM is best treated as local Gaussian submaps plus a global consistency layer. Agents optimize local RGB-D Gaussian submaps; inter- and intra-robot loop closures create constraints; pose-graph optimization updates global consistency \citep{thomas2025grand_slam,thomas2025grand_slam_arxiv}. Do not describe it as CoKo-style centralized rendered-depth fusion unless a source explicitly supports that.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{GRAND-SLAM sequential submap-pose-graph flow}
\label{alg:grand-slam}
\small
\textbf{State:} submap $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}=(\mathcal{M}_{i}^{m},\mathbf{P}_{i}^{m})$; pose graph $\mathcal{H}_t=(\mathcal{V}_t,\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t,\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{loop}}_t)$; stage tracker $s=0\rightarrow5$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{s=\arabic*},nosep]
  \item \textsc{Track}. \emph{Trigger:} RGB-D frame. \emph{Update:} update local pose inside active submap $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}$. \emph{Emits:} frame-to-submap pose.
  \item \textsc{Spawn submap}. \emph{Trigger:} $\operatorname{drift/extent}>\tau_\kappa$. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m+1}\leftarrow\kappa(z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}},\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m})$. \emph{Emits:} new Gaussian submap.
  \item \textsc{Local optimize}. \emph{Trigger:} active submap has support frames. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{M}_{i}^{m}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{photo/depth}}(\mathcal{M},z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}},\mathbf{P}_{i}^{m})$. \emph{Emits:} locally optimized submap.
  \item \textsc{Detect loops}. \emph{Trigger:} new submap/keyframe. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{loop}}_t\leftarrow\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{loop}}_{t-1}\cup L(\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}_{i,m})$. \emph{Emits:} intra/inter-robot loop edges.
  \item \textsc{Graph optimize}. \emph{Trigger:} pose graph changed. \emph{Update:} $\{\mathbf{P}_v\}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\mathbf{P}}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{loop}}_t}\rho(\|r_e(\mathbf{P})\|^2)$. \emph{Emits:} consistent submap poses.
  \item \textsc{Propagate}. \emph{Trigger:} optimized poses. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{G}_t\leftarrow\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{V}_t}\mathbf{P}_v\mathcal{M}_v$. \emph{Emits:} large-scale Gaussian reconstruction.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} Extracted results include Multiagent Replica ATE around $0.25\,\mathrm{cm}$ and Kimera-Multi Outdoor six-agent evaluation over about $1.85\,\mathrm{km}$ with average ATE around $4.99\,\mathrm{m}$ \citep{tian2023kimera_multi}. No public code was found in the research pass.

\subsection{MAGS-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:mags-slam}

\textbf{Mechanics.} MAGS-SLAM is the RGB-only outlier: each monocular agent builds local Gaussian submaps, transmits compact summaries, verifies cross-agent associations with geometry and appearance, and fuses via occupancy-aware Gaussian fusion \citep{cao2026mags_slam,cao2026mags_slam_project}. The core problem is scale-aware collaboration without active depth.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAGS-SLAM sequential monocular-fusion flow}
\label{alg:mags-slam}
\small
\textbf{State:} local $x_{i,t}=(\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{i,t},\lambda_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t})$; fusion $Z_t=(\mathcal{A}_t,\Lambda_t,\mathcal{O}_t,\mathcal{G}_t)$; stage tracker $s=0\rightarrow5$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\textbf{s=\arabic*},nosep]
  \item \textsc{Monocular track}. \emph{Trigger:} RGB frame. \emph{Update:} $(\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{i,t},\lambda_{i,t})\leftarrow f_{\theta}^{\mathrm{mono}}(z_{i,1:t}^{\mathrm{rgb}})$. \emph{Emits:} scale-hypothesized pose.
  \item \textsc{Local Gaussian}. \emph{Trigger:} pose estimate. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\leftarrow U_{\mathrm{rgb}}(\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1},z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgb}},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{i,t},\lambda_{i,t})$. \emph{Emits:} local monocular submap.
  \item \textsc{Compress}. \emph{Trigger:} upload/association window. \emph{Update:} $p_{i,t}\leftarrow\arg\max_{p\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{i,t}}\operatorname{info}(p)$ subject to $|p|\le b_i$. \emph{Emits:} compact submap summary.
  \item \textsc{Verify pair}. \emph{Trigger:} candidate summaries overlap. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{A}_t\leftarrow\{(p_a,p_b):G(p_a,p_b)>\tau_g\land A(p_a,p_b)>\tau_a\}$. \emph{Emits:} verified pairs.
  \item \textsc{Scale align}. \emph{Trigger:} verified association. \emph{Update:} $(\lambda_a,\lambda_b,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{ab})\leftarrow\operatorname{solve}(\mathcal{A}_t)$. \emph{Emits:} scale-aware transform.
  \item \textsc{Occupancy fuse}. \emph{Trigger:} alignment and occupancy evidence. \emph{Update:} $\mathcal{G}_t\leftarrow F_{\mathrm{occ}}(\{\lambda_i\Delta\mathbf{T}_i\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\}_i,\mathcal{O}_{t-1})$. \emph{Emits:} fused monocular Gaussian map.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Evidence and caveats.} MAGS-SLAM is the leading claimed monocular multi-agent 3DGS SLAM direction in this set, but available evidence is project-page/arXiv-stage. Code and data were reported as release-upon-acceptance, so runtime and bandwidth claims remain provisional.

\section{Architecture Taxonomy}

The synthesis treats multi-robot Gaussian SLAM as a factorization of four architectural responsibilities:
\[
\underbrace{\phi_i}_{\text{local tracking/mapping}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\mu_i}_{\text{communication/update selection}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\alpha}_{\text{cross-agent alignment}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\Psi}_{\text{shared Gaussian fusion}}.
\]
Here $\phi_i$ builds local map state from each agent's observations, $\mu_i$ selects what each agent communicates, $\alpha$ estimates transforms or constraints between local frames, and $\Psi$ fuses local states into $\mathcal{G}_t$. Existing systems differ less in whether they use Gaussians than in where these responsibilities live.

The benchmark evidence for online multi-robot Gaussian SLAM is promising but not yet comparable in the way mature visual-SLAM benchmarks are comparable. The strongest common benchmark surface is the Replica-derived multi-agent family: GRAND-SLAM, MAC-Ego3D, MAGiC-SLAM, and HAMMER all report variants of ATE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and depth error on Multiagent Replica or ReplicaMultiAgent. That overlap is useful, but it should not be mistaken for a fully standardized benchmark. The papers differ in dataset splits, sensor assumptions, baselines, runtime accounting, and whether the reported time includes tracking, mapping, global merging, rendering, alignment, or full end-to-end operation.

Accuracy and rendering quality are much more consistently reported than systems metrics. MAC-Ego3D reports per-frame tracking and mapping time; MAGiC-SLAM reports tracking/mapping time, global merge time, peak GPU memory, and submap disk size; HAMMER reports online update latency, alignment time, and per-device bandwidth; GRAND-SLAM reports strong large-scale outdoor accuracy evidence and hardware, but the extracted outputs did not contain a runtime, memory, or bandwidth table. Communication evidence is the biggest benchmark gap. HAMMER is the clearest system-level exception, while CoKo-SLAM and CoMA-SLAM report relative communication reductions without absolute byte counts or bandwidth tables.

Scaling evidence is also incomplete. Most Replica-style comparisons are two-agent indoor evaluations. GRAND-SLAM provides stronger multi-agent scaling evidence by reporting six agents over $1.85\,\mathrm{km}$ on Kimera-Multi Outdoor, while MAGiC-SLAM includes a three-agent AriaMultiagent setup and HAMMER reports heterogeneous hardware deployments up to four devices. These are important signals, but they are not a systematic scaling study over agent count, map size, trajectory length, update rate, and network load.

\section{Implications for Mosaic}

For Mosaic, the immediate lesson is to model contracts rather than import research code. The strongest backend candidates are CUDA/native-extension-heavy systems with dataset assumptions, ad-hoc configuration, and rapidly changing dependencies. A stable Mosaic core should therefore keep real Gaussian SLAM backends behind protocols, registries, optional dependencies, and isolation boundaries. The shared representation should expose typed snapshots, provenance, frame semantics, and diagnostics independently of whether the producing backend is server-centric, distributed, submap-based, monocular, or semantic.

The survey also suggests concrete API seams. Local backend updates should be distinct from fusion updates: $\phi_i$ and $\Psi$ have different state, timing, and failure modes. Communication units should be typed explicitly: keyframes, compact Gaussians, descriptors, local poses, semantic embeddings, and full submaps have different bandwidth and privacy implications. Cross-agent alignment should be represented as first-class constraints rather than hidden inside a map merge. Evaluation artifacts should include not only ATE and rendering metrics but also serialized update size, peak memory, per-update latency, global merge latency, alignment latency, stale-update behavior, and agent-count scaling.

HAMMER and MAGS-SLAM point to future agent-facing requirements. A shared Gaussian map may be queried not only for rendering or localization, but also for semantic labels, uncertainty, provenance, and task-relevant spatial evidence. Mosaic should preserve that path by returning bounded typed posterior views to agents rather than raw dense splat dumps or provider-specific prompts.

\section{Limitations}

This survey is constrained by the state of a fast-moving 2024--2026 literature. Some systems are available as public repositories, but public does not mean reproducible, licensed for reuse, or suitable as a library dependency. Some systems are target-class by paper claims, but not auditable as code. Communication numbers are especially uneven: relative reductions from CoKo-SLAM or CoMA-SLAM are not interchangeable with HAMMER's reported Mbps streams, and communication intervals such as $T_{\mathrm{comm}}$ are not byte counts. Runtime numbers are also not directly comparable because papers measure tracking, mapping, rendering, map merging, and inter-robot alignment differently.

The survey intentionally does not rank systems by a single score. A Mosaic backend shortlist would depend on the immediate objective: public-code experimentation, distributed-fusion design, heterogeneous semantic mapping, monocular collaboration, or outdoor large-scale consistency. The evidence is strong enough to define contracts and evaluation axes, but not strong enough to choose a production backend without additional reproduction work.

\section{Conclusion}

Multi-robot online Gaussian SLAM is no longer speculative: several systems now demonstrate shared Gaussian maps across multiple agents, and public research code exists for important RGB-D, consensus, compact-keyframe, and Gaussian-surfel variants. The field is still immature as a systems discipline. Benchmark tables emphasize pose and rendering quality, while the operational quantities that matter for robots---bandwidth, memory, latency, merge cost, scale, and fault tolerance---remain inconsistently reported.

For Mosaic, the practical conclusion is to use these systems as architectural evidence, not as dependencies to absorb wholesale. MAGiC-SLAM and MAC-Ego3D are the strongest public implementation baselines; CoKo-SLAM and CoMA-SLAM are important communication-efficient directions with reuse caveats; HAMMER, GRAND-SLAM, and MAGS-SLAM are key paper-only references for heterogeneous semantics, large-scale consistency, and monocular collaboration. Mosaic should encode the stable seams they imply: local mapping, update selection, cross-agent alignment, shared-map fusion, provenance, and bounded agent-facing posterior queries.

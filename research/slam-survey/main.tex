\section{Introduction}

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has quickly become a practical scene representation for differentiable rendering and dense visual mapping. In robotics, the hard question is not only whether a single camera can maintain a local Gaussian map, but whether multiple moving robots can contribute bounded online updates to a global map without collapsing under frame-alignment errors, duplicate primitives, network load, or implementation-specific runtime constraints.

This survey focuses on systems that satisfy a functional target: multiple independently moving agents, online or incremental operation, Gaussian or Gaussian-like local map state, a global fusion mechanism, and explicit cross-agent alignment. That target is narrower than ordinary 3DGS reconstruction and broader than papers whose title contains the word ``SLAM.'' It includes RGB-D multi-agent Gaussian SLAM systems such as MAGiC-SLAM and GRAND-SLAM \citep{yugay2025magic_slam,thomas2025grand_slam}, consensus-oriented systems such as MAC-Ego3D \citep{xu2024mac_ego3d}, communication-efficient compact-map systems such as CoKo-SLAM and CoMA-SLAM \citep{li2026coko_slam,chen2026coma_slam}, heterogeneous semantic mapping systems such as HAMMER \citep{yu2025hammer}, and provisional monocular multi-agent systems such as MAGS-SLAM \citep{cao2026mags_slam}.

\section{Setting the Stage}

Multi-robot SLAM is usually formulated as the joint estimation of robot trajectories and an environment representation from sensor measurements. Classical visual and visual-inertial systems express this through poses, landmarks, keyframes, and loop-closure factors; ORB-SLAM3 and Kimera-Multi are representative references for visual multi-map and distributed multi-robot evaluation practice \citep{campos2021orb_slam3,tian2023kimera_multi}. Gaussian SLAM changes the map variable. The global state is not only a sparse feature graph or occupancy map, but a differentiable set of Gaussian primitives whose geometry, appearance, visibility, uncertainty, and provenance may all change during online collaboration.

Let the set of agents be
\[
\mathcal{A}=\{1,\ldots,N\}, \qquad N\geq2,
\]
and let $t\in\mathbb{N}$ index local update time. The paper uses $\mathcal{F}_0$ for a global or server frame and $\mathcal{F}_{i,t}$ for the current local camera, body, or active Gaussian-submap frame of agent $i$. A rigid transform
\[
\mathbf{T}_{b\leftarrow a}\in SE(3)
\]
maps a point expressed in frame $\mathcal{F}_a$ into frame $\mathcal{F}_b$. Thus $\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}$ maps agent $i$'s local coordinates at time $t$ into the global frame. Local tracking estimates either this transform directly or a local-frame trajectory
\[
\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}
=\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,1},\ldots,
\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}\},
\]
where $\mathcal{F}_{\ell_i}$ is a local reference frame that must later be aligned to $\mathcal{F}_0$ by global tracking or global alignment.

The observation history for agent $i$ is
\[
\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}=\{z_{i,1},\ldots,z_{i,t}\}.
\]
A single observation is method-dependent. For RGB-D systems one may write
\[
z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rgbd}}=(I_{i,t},D_{i,t},\mathbf{K}_i),
\]
where $I_{i,t}:\Omega_i\rightarrow[0,1]^3$ is color, $D_{i,t}:\Omega_i\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}$ is depth, and $\mathbf{K}_i$ contains intrinsics. Monocular systems omit $D_{i,t}$ and introduce an unknown or estimated scale $\lambda_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}$. Heterogeneous systems may add local SLAM poses, LiDAR-derived depth, semantic embeddings, or device metadata,
\[
z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{het}}=(I_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},d_{i,t},e_{i,t},\xi_i),
\]
where $d_{i,t}$ denotes optional geometric support, $e_{i,t}$ an optional semantic descriptor, and $\xi_i$ a device or embodiment descriptor.

A Gaussian primitive is written as
\[
g_k=(\boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\mathbf{R}_k,\mathbf{s}_k,o_k,\mathbf{c}_k,\mathbf{h}_k,\pi_k),
\]
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\in\mathbb{R}^3$ is the mean, $\mathbf{R}_k\in SO(3)$ and $\mathbf{s}_k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}$ parameterize anisotropic shape, $o_k\in[0,1]$ is opacity, $\mathbf{c}_k$ denotes color or spherical-harmonic appearance parameters, $\mathbf{h}_k$ is an optional semantic or learned descriptor, and $\pi_k$ records provenance such as source agent, keyframe, or submap identity. A local map is a finite Gaussian or Gaussian-like set
\[
\mathcal{M}_{i,t}=\{g_{i,t}^{1},\ldots,g_{i,t}^{m_{i,t}}\},
\]
possibly partitioned into Gaussian submaps $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$, keyframe supports $\mathcal{K}_{i,t}$, or Gaussian-surfel submaps $\mathcal{S}_{i,t}$. A local tracking operator $\operatorname{local\_tracking}_i$ estimates $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}$ from observations and prior local state. A local mapping function is therefore
\[
\operatorname{local\_mapping}_i:\bigl(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t}\bigr)
\mapsto \mathcal{M}_{i,t},
\]
where $\mathcal{P}_{i,t}$ denotes method parameters such as insertion thresholds, photometric losses, depth weights, pruning rules, and keyframe policies.

The terminology in the remainder of the paper follows a local/global split. A \emph{local map} is the full Gaussian state $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ maintained by agent $i$ before cross-agent alignment. A \emph{Gaussian submap} is a bounded partition $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}$ of a local map, while a \emph{Gaussian-surfel submap} is denoted $\mathcal{S}_{i,t}$ when a method uses surfel primitives. The \emph{global map} is always $\mathcal{G}_t$, whether it is held by a central server or distributed across agents. Local tracking and local fusion update $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}$ and $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$; global tracking, global alignment, and global fusion estimate cross-agent transforms and update $\mathcal{G}_t$.

Online collaboration requires bounded communication. Let
\[
p_{i,t}=\operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)
\]
be a packet selected under budget $b_i$. Depending on the method, $p_{i,t}$ may contain keyframes, descriptors, compacted Gaussians, Gaussian-submap summaries, loop-closure candidates, semantic embeddings, or local pose fragments. The active communication graph is $\mathcal{H}_t=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{R}_t)$; $\mathcal{N}_t(i)=\{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_t\}$ is the neighbor set for distributed methods.

Cross-agent alignment is represented by a constraint set
\[
\mathcal{C}_t=\{c_{ab}\},\qquad
c_{ab}=(a,b,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b},\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{ab},\tau_{ab}),
\]
where $\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b}\in SE(3)$ is a relative transform, $\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{ab}$ is an information matrix or confidence surrogate, and $\tau_{ab}$ records the time, keyframe, or submap support. Constraints may arise from image retrieval, rendered-depth registration, camera-depth registration, geometric verification, semantic correspondence, consensus factors, or inter-robot loop closure. A generic alignment operator is
\[
\operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t:\{p_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}
\mapsto \mathcal{C}_t.
\]

The global map is the output of a global fusion operator
\[
\mathcal{G}_t
=\operatorname{global\_fusion}_t\!\left(\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t_i}\mathcal{M}_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}},
\mathcal{C}_t,
\mathcal{P}_t\right),
\]
where $\mathcal{P}_t$ contains global policies such as merge thresholds, update rates, consensus weights, redundancy tests, semantic-code retention, and communication schedules. Pose-graph methods instantiate $\operatorname{global\_fusion}_t$ through an optimization over submap poses,
\[
\{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\}
=\arg\min_{\{\mathbf{P}_v\}}
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t}
\rho\!\left(\|r_e(\{\mathbf{P}_v\})\|_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}_e}^{2}\right),
\]
whereas consensus methods instantiate it through repeated local and inter-agent updates,
\[
\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}
=C_{\mathrm{inter}}\!\left(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)},
\{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\};\lambda_{\mathrm{inter}}\right).
\]

Finally, reported performance should be read as a vector rather than a single score:
\[
\mathbf{q}=\bigl(\mathrm{ATE},\mathrm{PSNR},\mathrm{SSIM},\mathrm{LPIPS},L_D,
\ell_{\mathrm{track}},\ell_{\mathrm{map}},\ell_{\mathrm{merge}},m_{\mathrm{GPU}},s_{\mathrm{pkt}},r_{\mathrm{net}},N\bigr).
\]
Here $L_D$ is a depth error, $\ell_{\mathrm{track}}$, $\ell_{\mathrm{map}}$, and $\ell_{\mathrm{merge}}$ are tracking, mapping, and merge latencies, $m_{\mathrm{GPU}}$ is peak memory, $s_{\mathrm{pkt}}$ is serialized update size, $r_{\mathrm{net}}$ is communication rate, and $N$ is agent count. Current papers report different projections of $\mathbf{q}$, which is why this survey emphasizes architecture rather than a universal ranking.

A system belongs to \targetclass{} when it satisfies five conditions: $N\geq2$ independently moving agents; online or incremental operation; Gaussian or Gaussian-like local map state; global-map semantics; and a cross-agent alignment mechanism. This excludes static multi-view 3DGS training, ordinary stereo, single-robot multi-camera rigs, and independent per-agent mapping without global Gaussian-map semantics.

The main algorithms below use named survey operators so that the comparison stays readable. \Cref{app:expanded-survey-operators} expands those operators into the tracking, mapping, communication, alignment, and fusion steps they summarize.

\section{Existing Works}

The following systems are summarized as survey-level procedures over the notation above. The algorithm boxes are not executable source code; they identify the main mathematical objects each method estimates, communicates, aligns, and fuses. System-specific tracking operators are used where a generic $\operatorname{local\_tracking}_i$ call would hide materially different front ends.

\subsection{MAGiC-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:magic-slam}

MAGiC-SLAM\footnote{\url{https://github.com/VladimirYugay/MAGiC-SLAM}} \citep{yugay2025magic_slam} is a server-mediated RGB-D pipeline: agents perform local tracking, update local Gaussian maps, partition them into Gaussian submaps, send features or Gaussian submaps to a server, and the server performs global alignment plus global fusion before merging those submaps. The central mechanism is not raw image fusion; it is \emph{Gaussian-submap plus loop-constraint} global fusion.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAGiC-SLAM}
\label{alg:magic-slam}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require RGB-D streams $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}$, local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1}\}$, Gaussian submaps $\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}$, keyframe sets $\{\mathcal{K}_{i,t}\}$
  \Ensure Global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$ and optimized Gaussian-submap poses $\{\mathbf{P}_v\}$
  \State $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t} \gets \operatorname{magic\_rgbd\_tracking}_i(z_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t-1})$ \Comment{see \Cref{alg:app-magic-rgbd-tracking}}
  \State $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t} \gets \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t-1}\cup\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}\}$ \Comment{trajectory extension}
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{local\_mapping}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$ \Comment{local map update}
  \State $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m} \subseteq \mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ \Comment{submap policy refresh}
  \State $p_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$ \Comment{server packet}
  \State $\mathcal{C}_t \gets \mathcal{C}_{t-1}\cup\operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t(\{p_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}})$ \Comment{verified loop constraints}
  \State $\{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\} \gets \operatorname{pose\_graph\_optimize}(\mathcal{E}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t)$ \Comment{submap pose graph}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \operatorname{global\_fusion}_t(\{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\mathcal{B}_v\},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$ \Comment{global submap merge}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Reported ReplicaMultiagent results include about $0.27\,\mathrm{cm}$ ATE RMSE, $34.26\,\mathrm{dB}$ PSNR, $1.12\,\mathrm{GiB}$ peak GPU memory, $54\,\mathrm{MB}$ submaps, and $167\,\mathrm{s}$ global merge time. These figures make MAGiC-SLAM a mature public research baseline, but they also show that global merging can dominate runtime.

\subsection{MAC-Ego3D}
\label{sec:systems:mac-ego3d}

MAC-Ego3D\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Xiaohao-Xu/MAC-Ego3D}} \citep{xu2024mac_ego3d} is a Gaussian-consensus system that replaces a single authoritative merge step. Each RGB-D agent maintains a local Gaussian map; nearby Gaussians are regularized within an agent, and asynchronous inter-agent consensus aligns and refines the global structure. The key object is the repeated consensus update, not a one-shot server merge.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAC-Ego3D}
\label{alg:mac-ego3d}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require RGB-D streams $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}$, local Gaussian maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1}\}$, communication graph $\mathcal{H}_t$, consensus round $h$
  \Ensure Consensus-refined local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}\}$ and eventually consistent global map $\mathcal{G}_t$
  \State $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t} \gets \operatorname{mac\_consensus\_tracking}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1})$ \Comment{see \Cref{alg:app-mac-consensus-tracking}}
  \State $\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{local\_mapping}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$ \Comment{provisional map}
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}} \gets C_{\mathrm{intra}}(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t};\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}})$ \Comment{intra-agent consensus}
  \State $p_{i,t}^{(h)} \gets \operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},h;b_i)$ \Comment{neighbor packet}
  \State $\mathcal{R}_{i,t}^{(h)} \gets \{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\}$ \Comment{asynchronous exchange}
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)} \gets C_{\mathrm{inter}}(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{loc}},\mathcal{R}_{i,t}^{(h)};\lambda_{\mathrm{inter}})$ \Comment{inter-agent consensus}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}:i\in\mathcal{A}\}$ \Comment{distributed global state}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Extracted results report about $0.14\,\mathrm{cm}$ ATE RMSE on Multi-Agent Replica, $40.04\,\mathrm{dB}$ PSNR, $0.02\,\mathrm{s}$ tracking, and $0.05\,\mathrm{s}$ mapping. Communication is reported as intervals rather than bytes.

\subsection{CoKo-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:coko-slam}

CoKo-SLAM\footnote{\url{https://github.com/lemonci/coko-slam}} \citep{li2026coko_slam} is a server-pattern system that changes what is transmitted: compact keyframe-optimized Gaussian/keyframe state instead of dense raw streams. The server uses compact map information, loop closure without an initial relative-pose guess, redundant-Gaussian pruning, and global optimization.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{CoKo-SLAM}
\label{alg:coko-slam}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require RGB-D local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\}$, keyframes $\{\mathcal{K}_{i,t}\}$, budgets $\{b_i\}$, previous global map $\mathcal{G}_{t-1}$
  \Ensure Compact globally optimized Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$
  \State $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t} \gets \operatorname{magic\_rgbd\_tracking}_i(z_{i,t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t-1})$ \Comment{inherited tracker; see \Cref{alg:app-magic-rgbd-tracking}}
  \State $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t} \gets \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t-1}\cup\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}\}$ \Comment{trajectory extension}
  \State $\mathcal{K}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{coko\_keyframe\_selection}(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t-1})$ \Comment{compact keyframe support}
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{local\_mapping}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$ \Comment{local map update}
  \State $p_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$ \Comment{compact packet; see \Cref{alg:app-update-selection}}
  \State $\mathcal{A}_t \gets \{(a,b):\operatorname{score}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t})\geq\tau_a\}$ \Comment{supported associations}
  \State $\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b} \gets \operatorname{estimate\_relative\_alignment}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t})$ for $(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}_t$ \Comment{relative alignment}
  \State $\mathcal{D}_t \gets \{g\in\cup_i\mathcal{M}_{i,t}:\operatorname{overlap}(g,\mathcal{G}_{t-1})>\tau_o\}$ \Comment{redundancy set}
  \State $\mathcal{U}_{i,t} \gets \{g\in\mathcal{M}_{i,t}:g\notin\mathcal{D}_t\}$ \Comment{surviving primitives}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \operatorname{global\_fusion}_t(\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\mathcal{U}_{i,t}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$ \Comment{compact global fusion}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The paper reports $85$--$95\%$ lower transmitted data, but the extracted evidence did not include absolute bandwidth. The public repository supports architectural inspection, but the available evidence is not sufficient for an independent reproducibility claim.

\subsection{CoMA-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:coma-slam}

CoMA-SLAM\footnote{\url{https://github.com/npu-chenlin/CoMA-SLAM}} \citep{chen2026coma_slam} is a distributed multi-agent Gaussian-surfel system: agents maintain Gaussian-surfel submaps, close intra/inter-agent loops, and run distributed keyframe optimization. Its representation is 2D Gaussian surfels rather than conventional 3D radiance splats, so it belongs in a broad Gaussian-map survey with that caveat.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{CoMA-SLAM}
\label{alg:coma-slam}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Agent observations $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}$, Gaussian-surfel submaps $\{\mathcal{S}_{i,t-1}\}$, neighbor sets $\{\mathcal{N}_t(i)\}$
  \Ensure Distributed globally consistent Gaussian-surfel map $\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{surfel}}_t$
  \State $\mathcal{S}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{local\_mapping}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$ \Comment{surfel submap update}
  \State $\mathcal{L}_t \gets \operatorname{candidates}(\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{S}_{i,t})$ \Comment{intra/inter loops}
  \State $\mathcal{V}_t \gets \{(k,\ell,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{k\leftarrow\ell},\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{k\ell},\tau_{k\ell})\}$ \Comment{verified factors}
  \State $\mathcal{C}_t \gets \mathcal{C}_{t-1}\cup\mathcal{V}_t$ \Comment{constraint update}
  \State $p_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\mathcal{S}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\mathcal{C}_t;b_i)$ \Comment{neighbor packet}
  \State $p_{i,t} \leftrightarrow \{p_{j,t}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\}$ \Comment{distributed exchange}
  \State $\mathcal{Q}_t^\star \gets \operatorname{pose\_graph\_optimize}(\mathcal{C}_t)$ \Comment{keyframe/submap optimization}
  \State $\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{surfel}}_t \gets \bigcup_i\mathcal{Q}_t^\star\mathcal{S}_{i,t}$ \Comment{aligned surfel state}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The AAAI evidence reports improved pose/rendering/geometric consistency and a $99.8\%$ communication-bandwidth reduction claim, but exact numeric tables were not extracted. The repository was public, but no top-level license was found.

\subsection{HAMMER}
\label{sec:systems:hammer}

HAMMER \citep{yu2025hammer} is a server-side heterogeneous semantic mapper. Robots/devices stream local SLAM poses and visual/perception outputs; the server estimates global frame alignment without prior relative poses and trains or updates a metric-semantic global Gaussian map. The distinctive output is not just geometry, but a semantic Gaussian map usable by downstream agents.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{HAMMER}
\label{alg:hammer}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Heterogeneous packets $u_{i,t}=(z_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},e_{i,t},\xi_i)$ and previous semantic map $\mathcal{G}_{t-1}$
  \Ensure Metric-semantic global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$ and bounded query views $v_t(q)$
  \State $u_{i,t} \gets (z_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},e_{i,t},\xi_i)$ \Comment{normalized packet}
  \State $\mathbf{A}_{0\leftarrow\ell_i} \gets \operatorname{estimate\_device\_alignment}(\{u_{i,\tau}\}_{\tau\leq t},\mathcal{G}_{t-1})$ \Comment{device alignment}
  \State $\tilde{z}_{i,t} \gets \mathbf{A}_{0\leftarrow\ell_i}\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}z_{i,t}$ \Comment{global-frame observation}
  \State $a_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{associate}(\tilde{z}_{i,t},\mathcal{G}_{t-1})$ \Comment{supported primitives}
  \State $(g_k,\mathbf{h}_k,\pi_k) \gets \operatorname{update}(a_{i,t},\tilde{z}_{i,t},e_{i,t})$ \Comment{semantic Gaussian update}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \operatorname{global\_fusion}_t(\mathcal{G}_{t-1},\{\tilde{z}_{i,t}\},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$ \Comment{server map update}
  \State $v_t(q) \gets Q(\mathcal{G}_t,q)$ \Comment{bounded query view}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Reported evidence includes Replica PSNR around $37.28\,\mathrm{dB}$, online updates around $15$--$38\,\mathrm{ms/frame}$, alignment around $36\,\mathrm{s}$ per new robot, and $30$--$35\,\mathrm{Mbps}$ per device in hardware tests. No public implementation was found.

\subsection{GRAND-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:grand-slam}

GRAND-SLAM \citep{thomas2025grand_slam} is best treated as Gaussian submaps plus a global consistency layer. Agents optimize RGB-D Gaussian submaps derived from their local maps; inter- and intra-robot loop closures create constraints; pose-graph optimization updates global-frame consistency. It should not be described as CoKo-style centralized rendered-depth fusion unless a source explicitly supports that.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{GRAND-SLAM}
\label{alg:grand-slam}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require RGB-D streams $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}$, active Gaussian submaps $\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}$, loop-candidate database
  \Ensure Globally consistent Gaussian reconstruction $\mathcal{G}_t$
  \State $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathcal{B}_i^m\leftarrow i,t} \gets \operatorname{rgbd\_submap\_tracking}_i(z_{i,t},\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m})$ \Comment{frame-to-submap pose; see \Cref{alg:app-rgbd-submap-tracking}}
  \State $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m+1} \gets \operatorname{spawn}(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m})$ if drift, extent, or support exceeds threshold \Comment{submap split}
  \State $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m} \gets \operatorname{optimize\_gaussian\_submap}(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m},\mathcal{K}_{i,t}^{m})$ \Comment{active submap optimization}
  \State $\mathcal{C}_t \gets \operatorname{verify}(\text{intra/inter loop candidates})$ \Comment{loop closures}
  \State $\{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\} \gets \operatorname{pose\_graph\_optimize}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t)$ \Comment{submap pose graph}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \bigcup_v\mathbf{P}_v^\star\mathcal{B}_v$ \Comment{global reconstruction}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Extracted results include Multiagent Replica ATE around $0.25\,\mathrm{cm}$ and Kimera-Multi Outdoor six-agent evaluation over about $1.85\,\mathrm{km}$ with average ATE around $4.99\,\mathrm{m}$ \citep{tian2023kimera_multi}. No public code was found in the research pass.

\subsection{MAGS-SLAM}
\label{sec:systems:mags-slam}

MAGS-SLAM \citep{cao2026mags_slam} is the RGB-only outlier: each monocular agent builds Gaussian submaps from its local map, transmits compact summaries, verifies cross-agent associations with geometry and appearance, and fuses via occupancy-aware Gaussian fusion. The core problem is scale-aware collaboration without active depth.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAGS-SLAM}
\label{alg:mags-slam}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Monocular RGB streams $\{\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t}\}$, local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1}\}$, Gaussian submaps $\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}$, compact-summary budget $b_i$
  \Ensure Scale-aligned collaborative global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$
  \State $(\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},\lambda_{i,t}) \gets \operatorname{mags\_monocular\_tracking}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1})$ \Comment{see \Cref{alg:app-mags-monocular-tracking}}
  \State $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t} \gets \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t-1}\cup\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}\}$ \Comment{trajectory extension}
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{local\_mapping}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$ \Comment{local map update}
  \State $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m} \subseteq \mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ \Comment{active submap refresh}
  \State $p_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$ \Comment{compact summary}
  \State $\mathcal{A}_t \gets \operatorname{verify}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t};\text{geometry, appearance, occupancy})$ \Comment{accepted candidates}
  \State $(\lambda_i^\star,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b}^\star) \gets \operatorname{scale\_aware\_alignment}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t})$ \Comment{scale-aware alignment}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \operatorname{occupancy\_fusion}(\{\lambda_i^\star\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}_i,\mathcal{O}_{t-1})$ \Comment{occupancy-aware fusion}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

MAGS-SLAM is the leading claimed monocular multi-agent 3DGS SLAM direction in this set, but available evidence is project-page/arXiv-stage. Code and data were reported as release-upon-acceptance, so runtime and bandwidth claims remain provisional.

\section{Overall Architecture Taxonomy}

The systems above can be compared by the factorization
\[
\underbrace{\operatorname{local\_tracking}_i}_{\text{local tracking}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\operatorname{local\_mapping}_i}_{\text{local mapping}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\operatorname{update\_selection}_i}_{\text{update selection}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t}_{\text{cross-agent alignment}}
\quad + \quad
\underbrace{\operatorname{global\_fusion}_t}_{\text{global Gaussian fusion}}.
\]
The recurring question is not simply whether a method uses Gaussians. It is where the five responsibilities live, which variables cross the communication boundary, and which component absorbs drift, redundancy, and stale information.

\subsection{Central-Server Submap and Gaussian Fusion}

Central-server systems place most of $\operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t$ and $\operatorname{global\_fusion}_t$ on a global mapper. MAGiC-SLAM transmits Gaussian submaps and loop-closure evidence to a server that optimizes Gaussian-submap poses before merging Gaussians. CoKo-SLAM keeps the same broad centralization but reduces $p_{i,t}$ through keyframe-optimized compact packets and redundant-Gaussian pruning. HAMMER also uses a server, but its packet stream is heterogeneous and semantic: the server aligns local SLAM outputs into a global frame and updates a metric-semantic global Gaussian map.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Central-server submap and Gaussian fusion pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-central-server}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\}$, trajectories $\{\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t}\}$, packets $\{p_{i,t}\}$, previous global map $\mathcal{G}_{t-1}$
  \Ensure Server-side global Gaussian map $\mathcal{G}_t$ and transforms $\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\}$
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{local\_mapping}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$ \Comment{local update}
  \State $p_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$ \Comment{bounded upload}
  \State $\mathcal{C}_t \gets \operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t(\{p_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}})$ \Comment{cross-agent constraints}
  \State $\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\} \gets \operatorname{optimize}(\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t)$ \Comment{global-frame transforms}
  \State $\mathcal{D}_t \gets \{g\in\cup_i\mathcal{M}_{i,t_i}:\operatorname{overlap}(g,\mathcal{G}_{t-1})>\tau_o\}$ \Comment{redundancy filter}
  \State $\mathcal{U}_{i,t_i} \gets \{g\in\mathcal{M}_{i,t_i}:g\notin\mathcal{D}_t\}$ \Comment{surviving evidence}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \operatorname{global\_fusion}_t(\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t_i}\mathcal{U}_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$ \Comment{server fusion}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The advantage of this pattern is a coherent global optimization point. Server-side fusion can compare packets across agents, remove duplicate primitives, and maintain a single $\mathcal{G}_t$. The cost is that merge latency, memory, bandwidth, and single-server availability become first-order variables. MAGiC-SLAM's reported global merge time illustrates this cost, while HAMMER's hardware results are unusually explicit about per-device Mbps streams. Available ablation evidence is mostly about loop closure, alignment, compaction, or baseline comparisons rather than a complete systems ablation over bandwidth and agent count.

\subsection{Distributed Gaussian Consensus}

Distributed Gaussian consensus keeps every $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ active and uses peer updates to move those local maps toward consistency. MAC-Ego3D expresses this pattern most directly: intra-agent consensus regularizes each $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$, while inter-agent consensus uses packets from $\mathcal{N}_t(i)$ to refine cross-agent map agreement. CoMA-SLAM follows a related distributed idea with Gaussian-surfel submaps, loop factors, and distributed keyframe optimization.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Distributed Gaussian consensus pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-distributed-consensus}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)}\}$, graph $\mathcal{H}_t$, neighbor packets $\{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\}$
  \Ensure Consensus-refined local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)}\}$ and distributed global state $\mathcal{G}_t$
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)} \gets \operatorname{local\_mapping}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$ \Comment{local Gaussian state}
  \State $\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t}^{(h)} \gets C_{\mathrm{intra}}(\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h)};\lambda_{\mathrm{intra}})$ \Comment{local regularization}
  \State $p_{i,t}^{(h)} \gets \operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t}^{(h)},\mathcal{C}_t,h;b_i)$ \Comment{scheduled packet}
  \State $\mathcal{R}_{i,t}^{(h)} \gets \{p_{j,t}^{(h)}:j\in\mathcal{N}_t(i)\}$ \Comment{neighbor exchange}
  \State $\mathcal{C}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t(p_{i,t}^{(h)},\mathcal{R}_{i,t}^{(h)})$ \Comment{local constraint view}
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t}^{(h+1)} \gets C_{\mathrm{inter}}(\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{i,t}^{(h)},\mathcal{R}_{i,t}^{(h)};\lambda_{\mathrm{inter}})$ \Comment{consensus update}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This pattern reduces dependence on a single fusion server and can naturally support asynchronous collaboration. Its hard problems are convergence, conflict resolution, duplicate primitive handling, stale packets, and communication scheduling. Reported evidence supports strong tracking/rendering for MAC-Ego3D and large relative communication reductions for CoMA-SLAM, but byte-level communication, stale-update, and failure-mode ablations remain sparse in the available evidence.

\subsection{Submap-Plus-Pose-Graph Consistency}

Submap-plus-pose-graph systems treat dense Gaussian maps as local products whose global consistency is controlled by a graph over Gaussian-submap poses. GRAND-SLAM is the clearest example: RGB-D Gaussian submaps are optimized independently, loop closures add factors to $\mathcal{C}_t$, and pose-graph optimization updates the transforms that place Gaussian submaps into the global frame. MAGiC-SLAM also uses this pattern inside a centralized architecture, but GRAND-SLAM makes the Gaussian-submap consistency layer the main comparison unit.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Submap-plus-pose-graph consistency pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-submap-pose-graph}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Gaussian submaps $\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\}$, odometry factors $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t$, loop candidates, previous poses $\{\mathbf{P}_v\}$
  \Ensure Globally consistent Gaussian-submap placement and global map $\mathcal{G}_t$
  \State $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathcal{B}\leftarrow i,t} \gets \operatorname{rgbd\_submap\_tracking}_i(z_{i,t},\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m})$ \Comment{observation-to-submap pose}
  \State $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m+1} \gets \operatorname{spawn}(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m})$ if drift or support exceeds threshold \Comment{submap node creation}
  \State $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m} \gets \operatorname{optimize\_gaussian\_submap}(\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m},\mathcal{K}_{i,t}^{m})$ \Comment{submap optimization}
  \State $\mathcal{C}_t \gets \operatorname{verify}(\text{intra/inter loop closures})$ \Comment{relative-pose constraints}
  \State $(\mathcal{V}_t^{\mathrm{pg}},\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t) \gets \operatorname{graph}(\{\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}\})$ \Comment{pose graph}
  \State $\{\mathbf{P}_v^\star\} \gets \operatorname{pose\_graph\_optimize}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{odom}}_t\cup\mathcal{C}_t)$ \Comment{global consistency}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \bigcup_v\mathbf{P}_v^\star\mathcal{B}_v$ \Comment{overlap-reconciled map}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The strength of this pattern is that it reuses a mature SLAM abstraction: dense maps can remain local while global corrections act through $\mathbf{P}_v^\star$. The main risk is that a corrected pose graph does not by itself remove duplicate or inconsistent Gaussian primitives. A useful ablation surface is loop closure: GRAND-SLAM reports weaker results without loop closure, which supports the claim that cross-agent consistency factors, not only local photometric mapping, drive the large-scale result.

\subsection{Compact Communication and Monocular Submap Fusion}

Compact communication appears in both RGB-D and monocular systems, but it becomes structurally central for monocular collaboration. CoKo-SLAM uses compaction to reduce transmitted Gaussian/keyframe state while preserving enough information for server-side loop closure and pruning. MAGS-SLAM goes further: each agent has only RGB observations, so $\operatorname{update\_selection}_i$ must produce compact Gaussian-submap summaries that support both cross-agent verification and scale-aware alignment.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Compact communication and monocular submap fusion pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-compact-monocular}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Local maps $\{\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\}$, keyframes $\{\mathcal{K}_{i,t}\}$, scale hypotheses $\{\lambda_{i,t}\}$, packet budgets $\{b_i\}$
  \Ensure Bandwidth-limited global map $\mathcal{G}_t$ and verified cross-agent alignments
  \State $(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\lambda_{i,t}) \gets \operatorname{mags\_monocular\_tracking}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1})$ \Comment{local pose and scale}
  \State $\mathcal{M}_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{local\_mapping}_i(\mathcal{Z}_i^{1:t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t},\mathcal{P}_{i,t})$ \Comment{local map}
  \State $s(x) \gets \operatorname{info}(x)$ for $x\in\mathcal{K}_{i,t}\cup\mathcal{M}_{i,t}$ \Comment{packet scoring}
  \State $p_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{update\_selection}_i(\mathcal{M}_{i,t},\mathcal{K}_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t};b_i)$ \Comment{compact evidence}
  \State $\mathcal{A}_t \gets \operatorname{verify}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t})$ \Comment{geometry/appearance checks}
  \State $(\lambda_i^\star,\Delta\mathbf{T}_{a\leftarrow b}^\star) \gets \operatorname{scale\_aware\_alignment}(p_{a,t},p_{b,t})$ \Comment{scale-aware transform}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \operatorname{global\_fusion}_t(\{\lambda_i^\star\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t}\mathcal{M}_{i,t}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{C}_t,\mathcal{P}_t)$ \Comment{accepted fusion}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The tradeoff is between informativeness and bandwidth. Small packets help online operation, but if $p_{i,t}$ omits the wrong geometry, appearance, or occupancy evidence, $\operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t$ cannot reliably recover the transform. Monocular systems add scale ambiguity, so the alignment problem includes both rigid transform and scale hypotheses. CoKo-SLAM reports large relative transmission reductions; MAGS-SLAM is important architecturally but its code/data and quantitative communication evidence remain provisional in the sources used here.

\subsection{Heterogeneous Semantic Global Gaussian Mapping}

HAMMER expands the global map from geometry and radiance to metric-semantic scene memory. In the notation above, the Gaussian descriptor $\mathbf{h}_k$ and provenance $\pi_k$ are not auxiliary bookkeeping; they are part of what makes $\mathcal{G}_t$ useful to heterogeneous robots and downstream queries. The alignment operator $\operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t$ must reconcile device-specific local SLAM outputs, sensor modalities, and asynchronous packet timing before semantic Gaussian updates are meaningful.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Heterogeneous semantic global Gaussian mapping pattern}
\label{alg:taxonomy-heterogeneous-semantic}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Heterogeneous streams $\{z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{het}}\}$, local poses $\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}\}$, descriptors $\{e_{i,t}\}$, previous semantic map $\mathcal{G}_{t-1}$
  \Ensure Metric-semantic global map $\mathcal{G}_t$ with provenance-aware query views
  \State $z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{het}} \gets (I_{i,t},\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t},d_{i,t},e_{i,t},\xi_i)$ \Comment{heterogeneous encoding}
  \State $\mathbf{A}_{0\leftarrow\ell_i} \gets \operatorname{estimate\_device\_alignment}(\{z_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{het}}\}_{\tau\leq t},\mathcal{G}_{t-1})$ \Comment{device alignment}
  \State $\tilde{z}_{i,t} \gets \mathbf{A}_{0\leftarrow\ell_i}z_{i,t}^{\mathrm{het}}$ \Comment{global-frame packet}
  \State $a_{i,t} \gets \operatorname{associate}(\tilde{z}_{i,t},\mathcal{G}_{t-1})$ \Comment{supported primitives}
  \State $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\mathbf{R}_k,\mathbf{s}_k,o_k,\mathbf{c}_k,\mathbf{h}_k,\pi_k) \gets \operatorname{update}(a_{i,t},e_{i,t})$ \Comment{metric-semantic update}
  \State $\mathcal{G}_t \gets \operatorname{resolve}(\mathcal{G}_{t-1},a_{i,t};\text{uncertainty, support, semantics})$ \Comment{conflict handling}
  \State $v_t(q) \gets Q(\mathcal{G}_t,q)$ \Comment{bounded view}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This pattern is attractive because robot teams rarely have identical sensors or roles. A global map that contains semantics, source provenance, and queryable local evidence can support navigation, inspection, and human-robot interaction more directly than a purely photometric reconstruction. The open ablation surface is correspondingly broader: papers need to isolate the value of semantic descriptors, frame-alignment design, device heterogeneity, and communication choices rather than reporting only rendering quality or depth error.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

The main lesson from current multi-robot Gaussian SLAM is that architecture matters more than a single metric row. Local tracking, local mapping $\operatorname{local\_mapping}_i$, update selection $\operatorname{update\_selection}_i$, global alignment $\operatorname{cross\_agent\_alignment}_t$, and global fusion $\operatorname{global\_fusion}_t$ are separable responsibilities with different failure modes. A paper can report strong PSNR or ATE while still leaving open whether it scales in bandwidth, memory, merge latency, stale updates, or heterogeneous deployments.

A useful next step for the field is standardized systems reporting. Alongside ATE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and depth error, papers should report serialized packet size, communication rate, per-update latency, global merge latency, peak memory, map size, agent count, trajectory length, and behavior under delayed or dropped messages. These quantities should be separated by component: tracking should not be conflated with mapping, mapping should not be conflated with global alignment, and global alignment should not be conflated with final rendering quality.

The architecture taxonomy also suggests a modular research path. Central-server methods should be stress-tested for merge bottlenecks and server failure; distributed consensus methods should be evaluated under asynchronous and stale communication; submap-plus-pose-graph methods should report how dense Gaussian primitives are reconciled after graph corrections; compact-submap methods should expose the information lost by compression; semantic global-map methods should report provenance and uncertainty, not only photometric fidelity.

Multi-robot online Gaussian SLAM is therefore no longer speculative, but it remains immature as a systems discipline. MAGiC-SLAM and MAC-Ego3D are strong public baselines for centralized and consensus-oriented designs; CoKo-SLAM and CoMA-SLAM highlight communication-efficient directions with reproducibility caveats; HAMMER, GRAND-SLAM, and MAGS-SLAM expand the design space toward heterogeneous semantics, large-scale consistency, and monocular collaboration. Future progress will likely come from treating these patterns as composable architectural choices and evaluating each choice against the operational constraints of local-to-global robotic mapping.

\subsection{RGB-D Gaussian Tracking}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{RGB-D Gaussian tracking family}
\label{alg:app-magic-rgbd-tracking}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require RGB-D observation $z_{i,t}=(I_{i,t},D_{i,t},\mathbf{K}_i)$, active Gaussian map or submap $\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1}$, previous local trajectory $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t-1}$
  \Ensure Updated local pose $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}$
  \State $P_t \gets \operatorname{backproject}(I_{i,t},D_{i,t},\mathbf{K}_i)$ \Comment{colored point cloud}
  \State $\mathbf{T}^{0}_{t-1,t} \gets \operatorname{dense\_register}(P_{t-1},P_t)$ \Comment{frame-to-frame initialization}
  \State Freeze Gaussian geometry, opacity, and appearance in $\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1}$
  \State $(\hat I(\mathbf{T}),\hat D(\mathbf{T}),\alpha(\mathbf{T})) \gets \operatorname{render}(\mathcal{M}_{i,t-1},\mathbf{T}^{0}_{t-1,t}\circ\mathbf{T})$
  \State $M_\alpha \gets \operatorname{opacity\_mask}(\alpha)$ and $M_{\mathrm{inlier}}\gets\operatorname{residual\_mask}(\hat I,\hat D,I_{i,t},D_{i,t})$
  \State $\mathbf{T}_{t-1,t}^{\star}\gets \arg\min_{\mathbf{T}\in SE(3)} \sum M_{\mathrm{inlier}}M_\alpha\left(\lambda_c\lVert\hat I(\mathbf{T})-I_{i,t}\rVert_1+(1-\lambda_c)\lVert\hat D(\mathbf{T})-D_{i,t}\rVert_1\right)$
  \State \Return $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\ell_i\leftarrow i,t}\gets\operatorname{compose}(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{i,1:t-1},\mathbf{T}_{t-1,t}^{\star})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\Cref{alg:app-magic-rgbd-tracking} summarizes the MAGiC-SLAM tracking family and CoKo-SLAM's inherited tracker. The defining property is that pose refinement uses rendered color and rendered depth from a frozen Gaussian map, with masks for unreliable pixels, rather than updating Gaussians inside the tracking objective \citep{yugay2025magic_slam,li2026coko_slam}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{RGB-D submap tracking}
\label{alg:app-rgbd-submap-tracking}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require RGB-D observation $z_{i,t}$, active Gaussian submap $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}$
  \Ensure Frame-to-submap pose $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathcal{B}_i^m\leftarrow i,t}$
  \State Initialize pose from odometry, constant-velocity motion, or dense registration
  \State Render color and depth from $\mathcal{B}_{i}^{m}$ under the candidate pose
  \State Refine the pose against RGB-D residuals over the active keyframe or current frame support
  \State \Return $\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{\mathcal{B}_i^m\leftarrow i,t}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\Cref{alg:app-rgbd-submap-tracking} is the generic frame-to-submap version used by submap-plus-pose-graph systems. It should not be read as claiming that all such systems use MAGiC-SLAM's exact masking, initialization, or optimizer.

\begin{algorithm}[H]

\subsection{MAGS-SLAM Monocular Tracking}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAGS-SLAM monocular local tracking}
\label{alg:app-mags-monocular-tracking}
\small

\begin{algorithm}[H]

\begin{algorithm}[H]

\subsection{Cross-Agent Alignment and Global Fusion}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Cross-agent alignment}
\label{alg:app-cross-agent-alignment}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Packets $\{p_{i,t_i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}$
  \Ensure Verified constraints $\mathcal{C}_t$
  \State Retrieve candidate overlaps by image descriptors, Gaussian summaries, semantic descriptors, or neighbor exchange
  \State Verify candidates with geometry, appearance, rendered depth, camera depth, occupancy, or consensus residuals
  \State Estimate relative transforms in $SE(3)$ or $\operatorname{Sim}(3)$ as required by the modality
  \State Attach confidence, information, timestamp, and support metadata to accepted constraints
  \State \Return $\mathcal{C}_t$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Global Gaussian fusion}
\label{alg:app-global-fusion}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require Aligned local maps or submaps $\{\mathbf{T}_{0\leftarrow i,t_i}\mathcal{M}_{i,t_i}\}$, constraints $\mathcal{C}_t$, global policies $\mathcal{P}_t$
  \Ensure Global map $\mathcal{G}_t$
  \State Optimize global or submap poses when constraints define a pose graph
  \State Merge, concatenate, or consensus-update Gaussian primitives according to the architecture
  \State Suppress duplicates using overlap, occupancy, support, or semantic conflict tests
  \State Optionally refine the fused map with rendering, geometry, or semantic losses
  \State Preserve provenance so later queries can identify source agents, keyframes, and submaps
  \State \Return $\mathcal{G}_t$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

# Multi-Robot / Multi-Agent Gaussian SLAM Benchmark Evidence

## 1. Short executive summary

Benchmark evidence for true multi-robot or multi-agent Gaussian Splatting SLAM is emerging but still uneven. The cleanest multi-agent 3DGS evidence I found comes from GRAND-SLAM, MAC-Ego3D, MAGiC-SLAM, and HAMMER, mostly evaluated on ReplicaMultiAgent / Multi-agent Replica plus a small number of real or outdoor multi-agent datasets. Across these systems, commonly reported metrics are ATE RMSE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and Depth L1; runtime, memory, bandwidth, and scaling are much less consistently reported.

Key quantitative patterns:

- GRAND-SLAM reports strong multi-agent tracking and rendering: on Multiagent Replica, average ATE RMSE is 0.25 cm and rendering is PSNR 41.35 dB / SSIM 0.99 / LPIPS 0.04 / Depth L1 0.41 cm; on Kimera-Multi Outdoor, it evaluates 6 agents over 1.85 km and reports 4.99 m average ATE RMSE.
- MAC-Ego3D reports two-agent RGB-D collaborative Gaussian mapping with 0.14 cm average ATE RMSE on Multi-agent Replica, 40.04 dB PSNR, and 0.02 s tracking / 0.05 s mapping per frame; it reports communication intervals but not bandwidth.
- MAGiC-SLAM reports centralized multi-agent RGB-D Gaussian SLAM with ReplicaMultiagent and AriaMultiagent, including peak GPU memory 1.12 GiB, sub-map disk size 54 MB, mapping/tracking around 0.7 s per agent, and global map merging 167 s.
- HAMMER is the clearest heterogeneous multi-robot semantic Gaussian Splatting system: 2 robots in ReplicaMultiAgent, real deployments with 2 Aria glasses plus 1-2 ground robots, Replica average PSNR 37.28 dB, runtime 8 min, online update 15-38 ms/frame, inter-robot alignment about 36 s per new robot, and per-device streams of 30-35 Mbps.

For Mosaic, the practical takeaway is that the benchmark surface should separate: (1) mapping/tracking accuracy, (2) rendering/depth quality, (3) online update latency/FPS, (4) peak memory and serialized map/submap size, (5) communication bytes/rates/message types, and (6) scaling over agent count, trajectory length, and heterogeneous sensors. Current papers often cover (1)-(2), sometimes (3)-(4), rarely (5), and inconsistently cover (6).

## 4. Notes on cleanly extractable vs noisy / unverified claims

### Cleanly extractable

1. Multiagent Replica metric rows are cleanly extractable from the collaborative 3DGS survey and individual system pages for GRAND-SLAM, MAC-Ego3D, MAGiC-SLAM, and HAMMER. The common metric tuple is ATE RMSE / PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS / Depth L1 / Time where available.
2. GRAND-SLAM scaling to Kimera-Multi Outdoor is cleanly extractable: 6 agents, 1.85 km evaluated path, 2x RTX 3090 hardware, outdoor RGB-D setting, ATE and rendering metrics.
3. MAC-Ego3D runtime is cleanly extractable at the table level: 0.02 s tracking and 0.05 s mapping per frame, plus communication interval settings T_comm = 150 and 60.
4. MAGiC-SLAM efficiency evidence is unusually clean: 0.71 s mapping, 0.69 s tracking, 167 s merging, 1.12 GiB peak GPU, and 54 MB sub-map disk size.
5. HAMMER has the cleanest communication evidence: 30-35 Mbps per device, depth 25-30 Mbps, compressed color about 2 Mbps, 5 GHz WiFi, plus online update 15-38 ms/frame and alignment time about 36 s per new robot.
6. GS-LIVO is not collaborative, but its runtime/memory tables are clean and useful as adjacent evidence for real-time Gaussian mapping under LiDAR-inertial-camera sensing.

### Noisy, incomplete, or unverified

1. Some systems are hard to verify directly through primary publisher pages because IEEE/Springer pages blocked or redirected; arXiv HTML and survey tables were more accessible.
2. Communication evidence is weak for most Gaussian collaborative systems except HAMMER. GRAND-SLAM explicitly points to future communication/memory compression; MAC-Ego3D reports communication intervals rather than bytes; MAGiC-SLAM describes sending features/submaps but not bandwidth.
3. Memory/map-size reporting is sparse. MAGiC-SLAM reports peak GPU memory and sub-map disk size; GS-LIVO reports memory in adjacent single-robot tables; GRAND-SLAM, MAC-Ego3D, and HAMMER did not expose explicit memory/map-size values in accessible snippets.
4. Agent-count scaling is often demonstrated but not stress-tested. Replica-style tables are typically two-agent; GRAND-SLAM has a stronger 6-agent outdoor benchmark; MAGiC-SLAM has a three-agent AriaMultiagent setup; HAMMER has heterogeneous hardware deployments up to four devices.
5. Dataset public availability for derived multi-agent splits such as ReplicaMultiAgent, AriaMultiagent, GRACO, INS, DES, S3E, SubT-MRS, and DiTer++ needs follow-up verification against primary dataset pages or repositories.
6. Adjacent systems such as MCGS-SLAM, MM3DGS, Gaussian-LIC, and GS-LIVO should not be represented as multi-robot evidence; they are included only for context on multi-camera/multi-sensor runtime, memory, and modality.
7. Survey table values may normalize or re-run systems under a common setup; when exact reproduction matters, Mosaic should prefer primary paper tables and code artifacts over secondary survey aggregations.

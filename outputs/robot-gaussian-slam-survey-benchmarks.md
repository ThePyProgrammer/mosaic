## Prose interpretation for the survey

The benchmark evidence for online multi-robot Gaussian SLAM is promising but not yet comparable in the way mature visual-SLAM benchmarks are comparable. The strongest common benchmark surface is the Replica-derived multi-agent family: GRAND-SLAM, MAC-Ego3D, MAGiC-SLAM, and HAMMER all report variants of ATE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and depth error on Multiagent Replica or ReplicaMultiAgent. That overlap is useful, but it should not be mistaken for a fully standardized benchmark. The papers differ in dataset splits, sensor assumptions, baselines, runtime accounting, and whether the reported time includes tracking, mapping, global merging, rendering, alignment, or full end-to-end operation.

Accuracy and rendering quality are much more consistently reported than systems metrics. ATE RMSE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and Depth L1 appear repeatedly across the strongest target-class systems. In contrast, runtime, memory, map size, and communication are uneven. MAC-Ego3D reports per-frame tracking and mapping time; MAGiC-SLAM reports tracking/mapping time, global merge time, peak GPU memory, and submap disk size; HAMMER reports online update latency, alignment time, and per-device bandwidth; GRAND-SLAM reports strong large-scale outdoor accuracy evidence and hardware, but the extracted outputs did not contain a runtime, memory, or bandwidth table.

Communication evidence is the biggest benchmark gap. HAMMER is the clearest system-level exception, with reported per-device streams of 30--35 Mbps, depth at 25--30 Mbps, and compressed color around 2 Mbps. CoKo-SLAM and CoMA-SLAM report relative communication reductions of 85--95% and 99.8%, respectively, but the existing research outputs did not include absolute byte counts or bandwidth tables. MAC-Ego3D reports communication intervals rather than transmitted bytes, while MAGiC-SLAM and GRAND-SLAM describe submap, feature, or loop-closure exchange without supported bitrate or message-size numbers in the extracted evidence. For a robotics survey, this means bandwidth claims should be presented as paper-reported and method-specific, not normalized across systems.

Scaling evidence is also incomplete. Most Replica-style comparisons are two-agent indoor evaluations. GRAND-SLAM provides stronger multi-agent scaling evidence by reporting six agents over 1.85 km on Kimera-Multi Outdoor, while MAGiC-SLAM includes a three-agent AriaMultiagent setup and HAMMER reports heterogeneous hardware deployments up to four devices. These are important signals, but they are not a systematic scaling study over agent count, map size, trajectory length, update rate, and network load.

The survey should separate target-class multi-agent systems from adjacent single-agent or multi-sensor Gaussian SLAM. GS-LIVO provides useful runtime and memory numbers for real-time LiDAR-inertial-visual Gaussian mapping, but it is not multi-robot communication evidence. Similarly, multi-camera rigs and single-platform LiDAR-visual-inertial systems inform sensor-fusion contracts and resource envelopes, but they do not evaluate shared-map fusion, inter-robot frame alignment, or distributed communication.

A fair benchmark narrative is therefore: current papers demonstrate that multi-agent Gaussian maps can outperform older collaborative neural or sparse SLAM baselines on controlled indoor scenes and can extend to heterogeneous or outdoor settings, but the field lacks a shared evaluation protocol for online systems costs. Future benchmark tables should require not only ATE and rendering metrics, but also peak GPU memory, serialized submap/map size, per-update latency, global merge/alignment latency, bytes per update, bandwidth under realistic network links, and performance as the number of agents increases.

## Claims to keep explicitly qualified

- Treat all numeric values above as reported by papers, project pages, repositories, or survey-extraction outputs, not as reproduced results.
- Do not compare single FPS numbers unless the original papers measure the same operation; tracking time, mapping time, rendering time, global map merging, and robot-frame alignment are different quantities.
- Do not present relative communication reductions from CoKo-SLAM or CoMA-SLAM as absolute bandwidth savings without the missing byte-rate context.
- Do not treat MAGS-SLAM, MANG-SLAM, or CoMA-SLAM implementation details as equally mature as MAGiC-SLAM or MAC-Ego3D code evidence; the existing outputs mark MAGS-SLAM as promised-only, MANG-SLAM as mostly bibliographic, and CoMA-SLAM as public but license-unresolved.
- Do not count adjacent single-robot Gaussian SLAM systems as multi-robot evidence unless the paper explicitly evaluates independent agents contributing to a shared or fused Gaussian map.
